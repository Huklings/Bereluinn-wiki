# The Ackerdean

A fertile farming valley sheltered behind the lush coast, dotted with ruins from every age the land has been settled. At its southern edge stands **Aldbury**, an ancient city partly restored, where people ride in palanquins to keep clear of the pickpockets, and where the dwarf clans hold their family councils in an old hall. Off the coast, south of the city, lies an island that once belonged to it, half sunk into the water: the drowned spire of a betrayer god. Area 9, from an old draft (added 25 Oct 2026).

## The name
- **The Ackerdean:** Old English *æcer* "field" + *denu* "valley", the field valley. The breadbasket's own tongue ([[Languages and Naming#The Levont's human tongues (template, 19 Oct)]]).
- **Aldbury:** Old English *eald burh*, "the old fort". Everyone calls it that; its elden name is lost to all but archivists.
- **Sinkholm:** the sunken island. *-holm* is the river-trade country's Norse word for an islet, since the estuary's boatmen named it. The elves call the spire *Dorchenthúrel*, "dark tower" (*dorchen* + *túrel*, softened).

## Where
**Slot D**, between the plains and the central basin, running south toward the lush coast (slot 1) and the estuary at Cumar ([[Levont Area Map]], [[Rivers of the Levont]]). About 112 km from the capital. Its water drains south to the estuary, so its grain goes to Comber and from there up the railroad to the capital.

It fits the **breadbasket** of the [[Former Countries of the Levont|former countries]], the Anglo-Saxon-speaking farm country with the most people in it.

**The western larder (28 Oct).** The Ackerdean is the farm country of the west. On the far side of the Levont, the Wendfolk's [[Wendmark]] grows too.

## The valley
- **Farms and convoys.** Long waggon convoys roll through all year. The railroad works wonders, but not every town has a station ([[The Railroad#Station charters (decided 25 Oct)]]). Towns the line passed by send their grain by road, and the carters' convoys are their lifeline, and their grudge against the council.
- **Ruins everywhere.** Farmers plough up elden stones. Some ruins are lived in again. Others lead down into deep dark places that few have gone into and fewer came back from.

## Aldbury
An ancient city at the valley's southern edge, older than the old countries, partly restored and lived in.
- **Palanquins.** In the old quarters the well-off travel by palanquin, carried above the crowds, to keep away from the pickpockets and thieves' gangs in the dark alleys. The litter-bearers have their own guild, and hear everything.
- **Thieves' gangs** work the alleys. Are they tied to the famous guild under the valley lord's garrison in Area 5, or rivals of it ([[areas of the Levont]])?
- **The old council hall,** restored, is used by the dwarf clans (below).
- **Who built it:** the city was the betrayer's city. The spire on Sinkholm was its heart, and Aldbury its mainland half.

## Thrundrom, the oath hall
The restored council hall in Aldbury, where the dwarf clans meet for family councils. Dwarvish *thrun* "oath" + *drom* "hall" ([[Languages and Naming#Dwarvish (direction and roots approved, 19 Oct)]]).
- **Feuds are banned by oath** inside it, sworn before [[Verdan Ironoath]]. It's the one place every clan can meet: the Veingold and their debtors, the Ironhearth exiles, even the rare Oathbound envoy.
- **The [[Kharundrom]]** holds its meetings here.
- **A killing in the hall** would set all thirteen clans against each other ([[Dwarves]]).
- **Why humans gave it to the dwarves:** an open question. A gift at the founding? A sale to the Veingold?

## Hidden: the drowned spire (not for players)
Just south of Aldbury's ruins lies **Sinkholm**, an island that was part of the city, now half sunk and drowning. It was **a stronghold of one of the betrayer gods**, a wizard-lord who fought against the tapestry plan and ascended anyway ([[The Gods of Desirenthropy]], [[Sides of the Sundering]]).
- **The spire is sinking** into the ground and the water, and the drowned city around it with it. It sank when the Sundering ended. Some say the winners drowned it. Some say it sank itself to hide.
- **The water is dropping.** Each year a little more of the spire shows. Something wants it found.
- **A thin place.** The veil to the betrayer's tapestry is thin here ([[The Veil]]). Fishermen who sail too close stop sleeping. Greed and grudges spread along the nearby shore.
- **The lush coast's trouble comes from here.** The ruined temples of Area 1, full of dark items, and the "fallen beings" stirring up its warbands may be the spire's influence, leaking along the coast ([[areas of the Levont]], Area 1).

## Hooks
- A convoy arrives with one waggon fewer than it left with, and the carters won't say why.
- A ploughman breaks into a ruin under his field and doesn't come back up.
- A Thrundrom council is called after a dwarf is found dead in Aldbury, just outside the hall.
- Low water: a boat lands on Sinkholm for the first time in living memory.
