# Ruined Cities of the Veiled Frontier

The empty cities of the [[The Veiled Frontier|Veiled Frontier]]. The dwarves lost their holds in their own ways ([[Dwarves#The holds (decided 25 Oct)]], [[Dwarves#Hidden holds (decided 9 Nov)]]); humans and elves have causes of their own.

**Too few, not uncaring (decided 9 Nov).** Dwarves and elves both want their old places back. What stops them is that they are too few to retake and fill them.

## Human cities: most starved, a few were battlefields (decided 9 Nov)
- **Most starved.** The great elden human cities were fed by the wizard-lords' magic. When the gods broke [[Magic]] at the end of [[The Sundering]], the land could feed far fewer people, and the cities emptied over two or three generations rather than in one blow.
- **A few were battlefields.** Some cities were wrecked in the Sundering itself. These are the dangerous ruins.
- **This is the Comers' story.** The Comers who came through the March gate and by sea, about 550 to 450 years ago, fleeing "lands the Sundering ruined" ([[Timeline]]), were the people of these cities.

### What follows (suggestions, not canon)
- **What fed them:** rain called on time, fields with three harvests, canals that ran uphill. Without magic the land fed perhaps a third as many.
- **Who left first** (Fantasy Forge, disasters sort people): the rich by sea, with their gold; the poor on foot through the March gate. *"We came by the ship-road"* (old money) against *"We came with the gate-dust on us"* (proud of hard roots).
- **What's there now:** great walls round a shrunken town living in one quarter, the rest pasture and stone. Old magic-works half-running (a canal, a rain-tower). Over the old centres, and over the battlefield cities above all, the veil is thin ([[The Veil#What thins the veil]], working idea), which is why nobody rebuilds the heart.
- **The other side:** the gods' faithful call it the price of saving the world, since the wizard-lords were burning the land for their creations. Frontier dukes in the shrunken towns say the gods starved their grandfathers and called it mercy, a quiet seed for desirenthropy cults.
- **Folklore:** the last lord of a starving city had the rain-tower worked one last time, and the rain came black. Some old Levont families keep a handful of earth "from the city" none of them has seen.
- **Hooks:** a Levont great house claims a Frontier city's old title, and the duke living in its one quarter refuses; a rain-tower still works, and could feed a duchy while thinning the veil over it; a scholar visits her family's founding city and finds its people tell the Comers as the villains who fled with the grain.
- **Borrowing check:** cities failing after magic fails echoes "fall of the magocracy" stories in general (and D&D's Spellplague), with no close match to one work. Low risk.

## Elven cities: waiting cities and grey cities (decided 9 Nov)
- **Why they emptied.** Elven numbers are capped: a child is born only when a soul waits for rebirth, and with 400-year lives births are rare ([[Death and the Afterlife#Elves are reborn (decided 26 Oct)]]). The Sundering killed elves in great numbers; their souls went to the Waiting Wood ([[Athréth]]), and their cities emptied. Six hundred years on, they are still coming back a few at a time.
- **Waiting cities.** To elves these are not ruins. A few keepers hold them, empty, for people not yet reborn.
- **Grey cities.** The [[Northern Elves]]' old cities. Their dead can't be reborn, so nobody is coming home. They are truly dead.
- **The elves want them back.** They would reclaim their cities of splendour if they could, but like the dwarves they are too few.

### What follows (suggestions, not canon)
- **Lessons:** Tolkien's waiting promise (they mourn a golden age and wait, literally, for its people); Sanderson's third law (nothing new, only the rebirth cap, the Waiting Wood and the grey trees); Fantasy Forge history layers (a reborn elf may "remember" a street no living person has walked).
- **Limits and costs:** a handful of keepers can't defend a city, so a waiting city survives only by being left alone: remote, forest-hidden, or guarded by a thin veil onto the Waiting Wood (death doesn't stick, burnt ground greens again). Its cost is the living: young elves drawn from the Levont to keep a city they may never see filled.
- **Grey-city signs:** near them Athréth's grey shows through: no saplings where elves died, nothing regrowing.
- **The other side:** many Levont elves, especially the [[Aelnor]], call the waiting cities a beautiful folly ("the dead will be born where the living are"). Frontier dukes see empty land and treasure. Claimed memories are power: an elf who says "I remember this house" can claim it, and nobody can prove otherwise.
- **Folklore:** keepers set a lamp in a window each year for every soul they believe is on its way back. Elves say *"Kept, not lost"* and gently correct anyone who calls a waiting city ruined. Humans say that if you sleep in one you wake knowing someone else's name, and they'll come looking for it.
- **Hooks:** a reborn elf child in the Levont "remembers" being a lord of a waiting city, and three parties want to take her there; a Frontier duke's men start felling the forest round a waiting city and the keepers hire the party; a grey city turns up where no northern elves were thought to have lived.
- **Borrowing check:** empty elven cities are a genre staple (Tolkien's fading elves; Elder Scrolls' Ayleid ruins are dead, not waiting). Waiting for reborn owners is our twist. Low risk.
- **Still open:** where the waiting and grey cities lie on the map, and their names.

## The Frontier map (accepted 12 Nov)
![[Frontier rivers and cities.png]]

**Accepted 12 Nov:** you called the final map (with forests) "perfect". The rivers, lakes, forest zones, city places and landmarks shown are the working map. Names still marked suggestion below can change when the Frontier's languages are designed.

**Forest zones** follow real-world rules: the west coast and windward (western) slopes are wet and forested; the interior basin east of the central range is dry grassland with woods only along rivers; conifers on the mountains; Mediterranean scrub on the south coast; salt flats round the Salt Sea; the starved cities' old farmland round Bredham and Goldwic has regrown into forest (*"Under every oak, a furrow"*). Grey cities and Glassfield stay bare.

**Decided (your marks and picks):** the two northern forests (elven country); the **Indigo Outpost** area by the Adaria range; **Glaeshola** moved toward Adaria, near the outpost; three **ducal towns** in the north (unnamed); the confluence city is **Meinburg**, "my city"; the waiting city is **Coinneláth**, "place of candles" (11 Nov).

**Suggestion, not canon:** the river layout (Rivers A to E, unnamed until the Frontier's languages exist); the lakes; the other city names and places; the small landmarks.
