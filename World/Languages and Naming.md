# Languages and Naming

How names work across Beredluinn. This partly answers **14.15** ([[Worldbuilding Gaps]]). The general method (sound palette, roots, joining rules, wearing names down) is in the doc [Worldbuilding: The Levont](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/7901a3cd-349a-4a4a-95b2-b4078f9db570).

## Three layers (settled 19 Oct)
Species cut across the old borders ([[Worldbuilding Principles]]), so names can't follow species alone.

| Layer | Follows | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heritage tongues | Species | Given names, clan and house names, sacred things |
| Old-country vernaculars | Region | Everyday speech, most place names, nicknames |
| The Levont's common tongue | The state | Laws, council records, newer towns, official names |

A dwarf born in the river-trade country has a Dwarvish name and a clan name, plus a regional nickname everyone actually uses. Which name a person chooses to go by is a statement.

### Melting-pot rules
- **Registry shortening:** clerks write heritage names in the common tongue, and they get clipped and misspelled over time.
- **Translated surnames:** clan and house names get rendered into the common tongue. [[Bosurum Veingold]]'s "Veingold" is one of these.
- **Mixed families:** which name gets passed down, and who decides?
- **Names as politics:** going by your heritage name or your Levont name is a stance. This ties into the [[Aelnor]] split between restoring the kingdom and assimilating (10.14).

## Real-language skins (direction, 19 Oct)
Lean on real languages, the way Warhammer Fantasy does. Each human realm of the Old World wears the skin of a real language: the Empire sounds German, Bretonnia French, Kislev Slavic, Tilea Italian. Its dwarf tongue, Khazalid, was built on Tolkien's Khuzdul with a Germanic flavor. A reader can tell where a name comes from by its sound alone.

**Use it on layer 2:** give each [[Former Countries of the Levont|former country]] one real-language skin.

## The Levont's human tongues (template, 19 Oct)
Use this table as the template whenever Levont names are drafted. It's a working guide, not fixed canon.

All five old countries speak Germanic tongues. Old English, Old Norse, Old Frankish, Old High German and Gothic were real sister languages, so neighbors half-understand each other. That makes the union, and a shared common tongue, believable.

| Old country | Skin | Why it fits | Place-name endings | Personal names |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The old seat: **Reikmark** (named 2 Nov; people Reikmarkers) | Gothic. *reiks* "ruler" + *marka* "land, border": "the ruler's land" | Gothic is the oldest written Germanic language, so it suits the seat of law and archives | *-baurg* fort or city, *-gard* house or court | *-ric* (from *reiks*, "ruler"), *Theod-, Amal-, -mund*: Theodoric, Alaric |
| River-trade country (the Stromer) | German, with a Hanseatic flavour (changed from Old Norse, 2 Nov) | The river and toll towns of the Holy Roman Empire and the Hanseatic League; you wanted the Empire's feel | *-werth* river island, *-furt* ford, *-münde* river mouth, *-hafen* harbour, *-zoll* toll. Seat: Kaufwerth, "market island" | Trade surnames (Zöllner toll-taker, Fährmann ferryman, Schiffer boatman); great families take "von" and their seat (Ulrich von Kaufwerth). Given names like Konrad, Gertrud, Ulrich. *-son / -daughter* names now belong to the dwarves |
| Upland mining country | Old High German | German mining towns and miners' guilds | *-berg* mountain, *-stein* stone, *-burg* fort, *-tal* valley, *-bach* stream | *Gunt-, Hart-, -hard, -mar*. Surnames from mine trades |
| Breadbasket | Anglo-Saxon (Old English) | Old English place names are farm words. Farmers are most of the people, so this tongue carries the most weight | *-ham* homestead, *-ton* farm, *-feld* open land, *-ley* clearing, *-worth* enclosure, *-ford* | *Æthel-, Ead-, Wulf-* and *-ric, -wine, -stan* |
| Frontier march / old border kingdom | Old Frankish | "March" and "margrave" come from Frankish *marka*, border. Frankish frontier lords were the original margraves | *-court* estate, *-hem* home, *-march* | *Ch-* spellings like Childeric and Chlodwig, and *-bert, -wig* |

Gothic, Norse and Frankish are all Germanic, so all five stay in the template.

### How they mix
- **The common tongue:** Anglo-Saxon at its core, since the breadbasket has the most speakers. It picks up German trade words from the rivers and Frankish words for rank and war from the march.
- **Law and archives:** courts keep the old seat's words, the way English law kept Law French. A lawyer sounds like the capital.
- **Rivers keep Elvish names:** most English river names are older than English (Thames, Avon, Severn) and come from the Celtic tongues spoken before it. Elvish is now the Gaelic-flavored tongue, and elven cities stood in the marshes in the Elden Era (Area 4), so the oldest names on the Levont's map can be Elvish rivers and hills.
- **Order ranks:** the [[Order of the Rising Sun]] comes from the march, so its ranks can be Frankish-derived words: marshal (*marhskalk*, "horse-servant"), seneschal (*siniskalk*, "senior servant") and margrave. The humble original meanings make a nice contrast with how grand the knights are.
- **The six counties:** they were drawn across the old borders ([[Counties of the Levont]]), so a county can have Norse villages on one side and German ones on the other. The former raiders of the rocky coast (Area 2) would fit the Norse.

### Watch points
- **German and Warhammer (relaxed 2 Nov):** you enjoy Warhammer's Empire and the Holy Roman Empire feel, so German now covers the river country as well as the uplands, and **Reikmark** nods to Warhammer's Reikland on purpose. Keeping the breadbasket Anglo-Saxon and the march Frankish keeps the Levont five voices rather than one Empire.
- **Norse is left over** for the former raiders of the rocky coast (Area 2), if you want it.
- **Tapio is Finnish:** Finnish now belongs to humans outside the Levont. Tapio could have come in with a Finnic people abroad or a small minority, which fits a god whose faith crossed borders.

## Elvish (direction 19 Oct, kit approved 22 Oct)
Tolkien's elves plus Gaelic. They fit together: Tolkien built Sindarin's sound on Welsh, which is also Celtic. **The kit below was approved on 22 Oct**: method, sound palette, three rules, endings and roots, the same kind of starting kit as the Dwarvish below. Individual roots can still change.

### The method: a Gaelic skin with Tolkien's flow
Gaelic is a real language, so its words are free to borrow, the way [[Tapio]] borrows from Finnish myth. Tolkien's languages are someone else's work, so we take only their *feel*: soft, flowing, many vowels. The recipe:
1. **Start from a real Gaelic word** with the right meaning.
2. **Run it through the elvish filter:**
    - **Soften the ending.** Elvish words end in a vowel or in *n, l, r, s* or *th*. Gaelic endings in *-ch, -gh, -dh* or a hard consonant change.
    - **Keep the long vowels,** marked with an accent (*á, é, í, ó, ú*).
    - **Smooth hard starts.** Clusters such as *cn-* or *sl-* lose their first letter.
    - **Twist it until it's ours.** The result should hint at Gaelic, not be the Gaelic word. That's what you did with Dwarvish *ankhor* from Warhammer's *ankor*.
3. **Check it against Tolkien.** If it's a real Sindarin or Quenya word or name, change it. Words already rejected this way: *anor, galad, mellon, loth, maeth, dorath, rían*.

### Sound palette
- **Consonants:** l, n, r, m, s, th, v, f, d, g (always soft), c (always hard, never written k), h. Old and sacred spellings show softened sounds with an *h*: *bh* and *mh* (said v), *dh* and *gh* (soft, almost silent), *ch* (as in Scottish *loch*).
- **Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u, and long *á, é, í, ó, ú*. Favourite pairs: *ae, ai, ea, ia, ui*.
- **Signature sounds:** *l*, *n*, *ae*, and long vowels.
- **Never:** k, z, x, w, y, or harsh clusters like *kh* or *zh*.
- **Syllables:** mostly open (ending in a vowel). Words end in a vowel or *n, l, r, s, th*.
- **The contrast with Dwarvish:** Dwarvish forbids *e*; Elvish loves it. Dwarvish piles up hard consonants; Elvish softens them. Say a dwarf name and an elf name side by side and you hear two peoples.

### Three rules
Fantasy Forge's advice is one or two grammar rules, repeated until they're recognisable ([[Fantasy Forge Lessons]]). These three already explain the names in the vault.

**1. In names, the describing word comes first.**
*ael* (sun-warmed) + *nor* (folk) = **Aelnor**, "the sun-warmed folk". *fin* (bright) + *nor* = **Finelor**, "the pale folk".

**2. Softening: in a compound, the second word's first consonant softens.** This is Gaelic lenition, the habit that turns Irish *bean* into *mo bhean*.

| Hard | Softened | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| b | v (old spelling bh) | *ael* + *ben* (house) = **Aeloven**, "sun house", with a linking *o* (old spelling *Aelobhen*) |
| c | ch | *gaeth* (wind) + *cuaen* (bay) = *Gaethchuaen*, "wind bay" |
| d | dh | *coell* (wood) + *dúen* (hold) = *Coelldhúen*, "wood hold" |
| g | gh | *sen* (old) + *gléan* (valley) = *Senghléan*, "old valley" |
| m | v (old spelling mh) | *fin* + *muiren* (sea) = *Finmhuiren*, "bright sea" |
| t | th | *aird* (high) + *tóvar* (spring) = *Airdthóvar*, "high spring" |
| s | h | *glaes* (green) + *sola* (light) = *Glaeshola*, "green light" |

Words starting with a vowel, *l*, *n* or *r* don't change. Prefixes soften what follows too: *ne-* + *codhla* = *nechodhla*.

Old and sacred texts keep the *h* spellings. Everyday writing in the Levont uses the plain sounds: *Aeloven*, *Finvuiren*.

**3. Two n's never meet.** When a word ending in *n* meets one starting with *n*, the second becomes *l* and an *e* slips in. That's why it's **Finelor**, not *Finnor*.

### Examples
- **Nechodhlath**, "the place of no sleep" (*ne-* + *codhla* + *-ath*): what the elves might call the sleepless country (coined for the old working title, "the Sleepless Lands"; the world is now Beredluinn). Humans would wear it down to something like *Neckla*.
- **Coelldhúen**, "wood hold": a fort in a forest.
- River names (approved 22 Oct) are in [[Rivers of the Levont]]: the Mallon, the Dovan, the Rissan, the Solvan, the Pren, Darlon and the Aislan, with the estuary as Cumar.

### How humans wear the names down
Elvish names reached the Levont's humans second-hand, like Brittonic names reaching the English. Three things happen to them:
- **They shorten:** *Mahelavenn* becomes *Mallon*.
- **They get a second "river":** people who don't know *avenn* means river say "the Mallon River", which means "plain-river river".
- **They get folk meanings:** a farmer decides *Dovan* must be named after doves.

### Borrowing check
- **Gaelic** is a real language, so borrowing from it is fine.
- **Tolkien:** take the sound and grammar, not the words. Avoid lifting Sindarin or Quenya words such as *anor*, *galad*, *mellon*, *loth*, *maeth*, or names like Doriath and Rían. *-nor* echoes Quenya *nórë* ("people, land", as in Valinor), but Aelnor is canon and the echo is faint: about 5 on the [[Borrowed Ideas]] scale, Distant.
- **Possible collisions:** the roots were checked against Sindarin and against common Gaelic words, not every word in every language. Look out for anything that turns out to be a real word in a language you know.

### Dwarf personal names (decided 2 Nov)
Given name, a *-son* or *-daughter* name, and "of" the clan: **Bran Brennansson of Thrunoz**. An earned title name replaces the parent's name: **Bran Firebeard of Thrunoz**. Details in [[Dwarves#How dwarves are named (decided 2 Nov)]].

### Sound palette
- **Consonants:** b, d, g, k, kh, m, n, r, s, sh, t, th, z, zh, plus l only in old and sacred words (Dolumar).
- **Vowels:** a, o, u, i. **No e.** Any "dwarf name" with an e is a translation (Veingold) or a human mishearing.
- **Signature sounds:** kh, a hard rolled r, and the *-um* ending.
- **Never:** e, f, w, y, soft ch.
- **Clusters:** words can start with gr, dr, kr, khr or thr, and end in rm, rn, rk, nd or rd.
- **Syllables:** mostly heavy, closed CVC.

### Endings
These are our own, deliberately different from Khazalid's (*-az* for a thing, *-ak* for an idea).

| Ending | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *-um* | A person | Bosurum, Ishgadrum |
| *-umar* / *-ar* | A vessel, container or hold | Dolumar |
| *-ukh* | A place delved or worked | Gulmukh |
| *-oz* | A clan or folk | Gulmukhoz, "the gold-delvers". All thirteen clans are in [[Dwarves]] |

**Joining rule:** when a root names something worn or carried, a linking *-r-* comes before *-um* (Ishgad**r**um).
