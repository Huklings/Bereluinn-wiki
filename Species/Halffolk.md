# Halffolk

One small people living two lives: the **Wendfolk**. Pathfinder's gnomes and halflings are combined into one people. Most live in the country; some, the **Tonfolk**, moved from the Wends to the cities. They are no longer woodfolk. Decided 28 Oct 2026, named 29 Oct. **Everything under "Decided" is yours; the suggestions are not canon.**

## Names (decided 29 Oct)
- **Wendfolk:** the people as a whole. Every one of them comes from the Wends ([[Wendmark]]).
- **Tonfolk:** Wendfolk who moved from the Wends to the cities. Old English *tūn*, "town". The Tonfolk–Wendfolk split is a distinction only among themselves.
- **Talls:** what the country Wendfolk call the Tonfolk, from "gone tall".
- **Halffolk:** what everyone else calls them, without telling the two apart.
- **Wendings** is also a good name (an alias of [[Wendmark]]).
- "Halflings" and "gnomes" were our working words from Pathfinder and are retired (29 Oct). Pathfinder rules names such as Halfling Luck stay as they are.

## Decided (28 Oct)

### The two kinds
- **Wendfolk in the country.** They love farming and watching things grow. Their home is **[[Wendmark]]**, their own region with a few small towns and cities. Few outsiders visit, but food floods out of it in trade.
- **Tonfolk in the cities.** Wendfolk who moved to the cities from the Wends. Their tinkering led them into **alchemy**, built on herbs their country kin grow: teas, tinctures, pipeweed and brewing.
- **"Gone tall"** (perhaps): the country jibe for a Tonfolk who has grown away from the hearth.

### The luck
- **The luck is magic.** Halffolk passively pull magic, and it makes them luckier than others.
- **It is only luck.** It is not all-powerful and gives no visions. It tilts the odds; it doesn't point the way.
- **It runs on food.** Pulling magic takes a great deal of energy from the body, which is why halffolk eat so much. **A starved halffolk can even turn unlucky.**
- **It spreads to the whole people.** Outsiders tend to miss the paths to the shires ([[Wendmark]]). Wendfolk guides are how outsiders get in.
- **It can spill onto allies.** The rules wait for the homebrew; Pathfinder's Halfling Luck and Shared Luck feats are the starting point.

### Where the luck comes from (decided 29 Oct)
- **The luck draws on a tapestry.** Halffolk pull threads from one of [[The Tapestries]], like every other magic since the loose magic was woven away ([[Magic#How magic is restricted (decided 26 Oct)]]). It isn't a magic of their own outside the lore. Which tapestry is still open, and likely tied to whichever wizard-lord made them (11.12).
- **The luck goddess is a separate question.** She is simply one of the gods still to be created ([[Elven Pantheon]]), not a rival claim on the halffolk luck.
- **Cultivation (your idea, 29 Oct)** is a theme and word for them: they cultivate, and watch things grow. Now the name of their main god (below).
- **More than one god.** Halffolk can call several gods their own; their maker is only one of them (29 Oct).
- **Their gods (decided 29 Oct):**
  - **Their main god: [[Greenhand]]** (decided 29 Oct), goddess of Cultivation ("CG" for short). "Greenhand" is the name she goes by. Neutral in the Sundering, but went along with the mantle plan. A wizard-lord who looked on this small people and saw a promising future, something to *cultivate*.
  - **Why the luck exists:** CG made them, and during the great war gave them protection to grow and flourish, the way a gardener starts plants in a pot indoors before sowing them out in the wild. CG granted them nourishment and "luck" to survive. The war ended, and the luck remains. It draws on CG's tapestry.
  - **CG made them,** as other lords made their races ([[Pantheon#Where the gods came from (decided 26 Oct)]]).
  - **The luck simply remains.** It was a gift, and now it has grown into them. It isn't fading.
  - **Their hearth patron: [[Demerde]]** (decided 29 Oct), a minor god, an "it", living in Greenhand's tapestry. The name blends Demeter and *Erde*, "earth".
  - **The Tonfolk pray more to [[Verdan Ironoath]]** than to Greenhand. Oaths and the ledger suit them.
- **The hungers are separate.** Halffolk hunger and the [[Toad People]]'s hunger are not connected and shouldn't be read as an echo.

### Adding and subtracting
- **A moral philosophy, not a magic rule.** Halffolk judge life by what adds and what subtracts. Growing things adds. **Killing subtracts**, so they dislike it. The luck doesn't care; this is something they believe and enjoy.
- **The Tonfolk ledger.** Some Tonfolk keep a literal ledger of what they have added and subtracted in life.
- **Hospitality.** Halffolk feed guests and try to leave things better than they found them. That is why so many of the Levont's other peoples approve of them.

### Work and trades
- **Brewing.** The halffolk and the [[Dwarves]] compete over ale. Dwarf brews are dark and thick, like stouts. Elves brew ales and wine too, and humans brew wine and ale but aren't as renowned.
- **Tonfolk messengers** are valuable: their luck helps them arrive on time and find the person.
- **Detectives are rare.** A halffolk makes a fine investigator, but conflict usually subtracts, so few want the work.

## Suggestions (not canon)
- **Wendfolk ale is gruit.** Medieval ale flavoured with herb mixes (bog myrtle, yarrow, heather) before hops: light, herbal, different each season. Dwarves call it "tea with ambition."
- **A brewing contest** in one of Wendmark's border market towns, with a disputed judging as an easy hook.
- **Luck pools.** Many well-fed halffolk together add up. That would explain Wendmark's wandering roads, and make Tonfolk quarters a little harder to find your way through if you don't belong.
- **Famine opens the roads.** In a bad harvest the luck thins, the paths stop turning outsiders away, and Wendmark is visible exactly when it is weakest.
- **Luck as a class marker.** A poor, half-fed Tonfolk in the city is unlucky, and others may treat them as a bad omen.
- **Hungry soldiers are a liability.** Armies may refuse halffolk recruits, or halffolk units demand double rations.
- **Sailors' superstition.** A ship without a halffolk aboard is ill-fated ([[Navies of the Levont]]).
- **Courier houses.** Fed messengers are fast messengers, so a courier service needs stocked waystations. Emptying or poisoning a larder is how you stop one.
- **Guest-right.** No harm under a halffolk roof. A murder at a Wendmark inn would shock the whole Levont.
- **Neutral ground.** Elves and dwarves can sit at one halffolk table, perhaps even about [[The Channels]].
- **Liked, not respected.** "Charming little folk" is condescension the halffolk notice.
- **Bloodmonth.** Whatever the slaughter month is called, the Wendfolk would call it the Larder-month ([[Calendar]]).
- **Two halffolk competing:** the lucks cancel out, so contests between them are fair.
