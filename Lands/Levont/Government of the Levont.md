# Government of the Levont

The Levont is a union of six counties under a council. The counties hold the real power. The council is mostly a referee between them, seated in the capital of the old country that brokered the union.

How rule works below the council (land, nobles, bailiffs, churches and monasteries) is being worked out in [[Land, Lordship and Church]].

## Decided
- **Six counties,** drawn at the founding deliberately across the old borders. The old countries survive as grudges, family ties and dialects, not on the map. See [[Counties of the Levont]].
- **The council** has one seat per county, plus seats for the great institutions: churches, guilds, and perhaps the [[Order of the Rising Sun]]. Region against institution is the daily fight.
- **The capital** is the old seat of the country that unified the others: it brokered the idea of one larger country, to unify and defend them.
- **The capital is Neuhof** (named 2 Nov), "new court". Commoners wear it down to **Nuhof**; clerks and the council write Neuhof. It was new once, centuries ago, and the name stuck, the way Naples means "new city". Altdorf-style on purpose.
- **Where the capital is** (decided 22 Oct): in the crook of mountains in the Levont's northwest interior, opening onto a small bay, with the heartland around it. See [[Levont Area Map]], slot H.
- **History** (decided 22 Oct, [[Timeline]]): the union is about 120 years old. A strong regency ran it for its first 40 years, and about 30 years ago an Engelbrekt-style rising was crushed or co-opted.
- **Great houses and titles** (decided 2 Nov): human power runs through great houses, one per old country ([[Great Houses of the Levont]]). County lords are **Landgrafen**, march lords **Markgrafen**, small landholders **Freiherren**. The council is formally **the Council of the Levont** (named 2 Nov). Its county lords elect its chair, as the Holy Roman Empire's prince-electors did; in practice it is always a Reikhart.
- **Strong counties.** County lords run their lands. The council mostly settles disputes between them. The Order's private army is one more lord among lords.
- **The council and the Order:** the council keeps a strenuous hold on the Order, and the Order avoids open rebellion because too many soldiers would come down on it (7.9).
- **Station charters** (decided 25 Oct): every railroad station is a council grant, and routing the lines is how the council pays its loyalists. See [[The Railroad#Station charters (decided 25 Oct)]].
- **The horde as common sense** (decided 25 Oct): the union exists to face the horde of [[Lorweir]]. If the horde stops being frightening, the union loses its reason. So the council keeps the fear alive: the plains parade, beacon drills, horde-scare pamphlets and sermons. Cultural hegemony in [[Worldbuilding Sage Lessons#Cultural hegemony]]'s sense. Now that the horde's khan is dead ([[Lorweir]]), the fear is either about to come true or about to look foolish.

## The council and the Veingold (decided 25 Oct)
The council lives partly on rent from the underground right, and the Veingold are its biggest tenant. That could make the referee the lender's creature, so three things keep the two in balance. Neither can break the other without getting hurt.
1. **The lease has an end date.** The underground right is leased for a fixed term (about 25 years; exact length open). As renewal nears, the Veingold must court the council, which could re-lease to another clan or a human guild. No deal is forever.
2. **The railroad is the council's second purse.** Station charters, rail fees and shard claims bring in money the Veingold don't touch ([[The Railroad#Station charters (decided 25 Oct)]]). The Veingold also need the rails to move their ore and coin.
3. **The Veingold need protection.** Their hold is the largest working hold inside the Levont. Against the horde, resentful clans, or a county eyeing their mines, the council's soldiers and law are all they have.

Other dwarf clans add weight: the Emberbeard keep the trains running and the Salthollow hold the salt ([[Dwarves#The holds (decided 25 Oct)]]).

## Suggestions (not canon)
- **The broker's heir presides.** The ruling house of the unifying country chairs the council as first among equals. The capital's pride and the counties' resentment both follow from that.
- **The council's real levers** in a land of strong counties are the things no county can own alone: the way stations, the [[The Railroad|railroad]], foreign relations, and the unrevoked wartime emergency powers.
- **Seats as prizes.** Which churches and guilds get a seat is itself political. A railroad guild with money but no seat, or a church that lost its seat, are ready-made grudges.
- **The [[Green Wardens]]' patron** is a councillor, probably from a county seat or a church seat.
