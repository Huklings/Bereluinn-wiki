# The Gods of Desirenthropy

The wizards who opposed the tapestry plan, lost [[The Sundering]], and ascended anyway. "The gods of desirenthropy" is a loose name on purpose: they are not one unified front, only gods who share a side of history. Against them stand [[The Lawful Accords]]. Individual gods of desirenthropy can also belong to [[Side Pantheons]], even alongside gods of the Accords.

## The name
**Desirenthropy** is *desire* combined with *entropy*: wanting that wears things down. Above all, these gods desire to wear down the accords that bind them.

## What we know
- **They ascended anyway.** They were already so powerful that [[The Lawful Accords]] could not stop them from rising to godhood.
- They opposed the tapestry plan because it limited their power and took power from those who had not reached it yet ([[Sides of the Sundering]]).
- They lost the war and had to accept the terms: tapestries of their own, like every other god ([[The Tapestries]]).
- They are still alive and pursue their own ends: domination, greed and others.
- **A stronghold:** the drowned spire on Sinkholm, off the coast south of Aldbury, was one of their strongholds in the Sundering. It is sinking, and the veil is thin around it ([[The Ackerdean]]).
- [[areas of the Levont]], Area 5: a restless spirit in an elden dwarven hold is tied to "the tapestries of domination and greed". Their influence leaks through when the veil thins.

## How the Levont sees them
- Elves call them **the betrayer gods**. The [[Northern Elves]] are the remnants of the elves who joined them.
- The Levont is a medieval society. Some texts about these gods exist, but most people know them by word of mouth, and retelling has made them **darker and crueler than they were**.
- They serve as **bogeyman gods**: the names parents use to frighten children and preachers use to frighten congregations.
- **Many evil factions align with them**, which doesn't help their reputation.
- The gap between the real gods and their folk image is a story in itself.

## Why they are gray, not evil
- Their original cause was fairness: power should stay within reach of everyone.
- Their methods and their domains (domination, greed) are where they turn dark.
- Mortals who hit the gods' limits on [[Magic]] may find their case convincing.

## The gods (31 Oct)
Some are grey, some are hard to justify or redeem.

### The Kind Hand (decided 31 Oct)
The god of ease, greed and domination, one move in three steps: he eases your burden, he keeps what he took, and then you need him. Absorbs the draft "Merchant" (greed).
- **What he does:** takes away whatever weighs on you (sleep, grief, pain, worry) and never gives it back; he hoards it. Whoever comes to depend on his ease becomes his. Relief becomes a debt, then a leash.
- **Domains:** Repose, Indulgence, Wealth, Tyranny. Alternates: Dreams, Void.
- **Tapestry, the Lull:** a warm, dim, endless afternoon of soft halls and still water. The smiling sleepers there are both his hoard and his subjects.
- **The Restful are his.** The relic in the Area 5 hold drains the region's rest into the Lull: he creates the need he answers. This ties the hold's "tapestries of domination and greed" to him.
- **Case:** real relief for people in real pain. **Shadow:** every kindness is a purchase.
- *Suggestion:* the restless spirit locked in the hold is one of his own, set to guard the relic, or a lord who took too much ease and now serves him.

### The Six-Eyed Raven (decided 31 Oct)
The heart of the losers' cause: magic should be open to all. Behind the Birdsong cult (below).
- **Sight:** he sees backward, forward and every possibility, but never a clear picture of *now*.
- **Domains:** Magic, Knowledge, Ambition, Time. Alternate: Dreams.
- **Near the thinning weave:** people have strange prophetic dreams of the past and future around them, which makes them ambitious.
- **Case:** he is right that the limits are unfair. **Shadow:** every vision is true, and none tells you what to do today.
- *Draft tapestry:* the Open Eye, where nothing is hidden or withheld.

### Still in draft
- **The Unraveller** (darkest): the entropy in the name. Decay, Destruction, Void, Change. Tapestry the Fraying: everything comes apart.
- ~~**The Crowned**~~: replaced 31 Oct by the horde's gods and the dragon gods (below).

### The horde's gods (decided 31 Oct)
Hard to redeem, by design: the horde are opponents ([[Lorweir#Orcs are opponents (decided 25 Oct)]]). Each horde people was made by its own wizard-lord. Names are placeholders.
- **The Bloodfather, maker of the hobgoblins,** the horde's nobles. *Domains:* Duty, Tyranny, Passion, Might. *Draft tapestry:* the Endless Muster, where everything has its rank and the fallen rise to serve again.
- **The Many-Mouthed, maker of the goblins.** Swarm, hunger, theft. *Domains:* Swarm, Trickery, Fire, Wealth. *Draft tapestry:* the Warren, where everything multiplies and everything is stolen. *Thin spots:* rats, fires, things go missing.
- **The Bloodhawk, maker of the orcs** (decided 31 Oct). The orcs are brutes who roam in tribes.
  - *Domains:* Freedom, Destruction, Might, Nature. Beasts are a core theme too (PF2e has no Beast domain; Nature covers it).
  - *Tapestry, the Red Peaks (named 31 Oct):* a savage mountain range of large, bloodthirsty hunters, savage and feral as only humanoids can be. Werewolves and their like roam it.
  - *Thin spots:* tempers shorten, mobs are easier to sway, and beasts turn more humanoid, but feral.

### Dragon gods (draft, 31 Oct)
The odds that a dragon or two became a god fit (31 Oct). Drafts, not chosen:
- **The Chain-Wyrm:** the dragon who took a mantle and ruled the enslaved dwarves; [[Ishgadrum]]'s old enemy. Wyrmkin, Fire, Tyranny, Might. Tapestry the Ash Throne: everything burns down to what endures, and what endures is owned.
- **The Storm-Wyrm (optional):** older, alien, vast. Air, Lightning, Freedom, Destruction. An endless thunderhead.


## Cults
### The Restful (decided 25 Oct)
A cult that promises the sleepless a night's sleep. It works, at first.
- **Who joins:** the lonely and exhausted people of thin-veil country, the people no one else helps. They are grateful, and the camaraderie is real.
- **What they're told:** rest is a gift, and the Restful share it.
- **The truth:** the sleep is paid for by a god of desirenthropy. Only the inner circle knows which one, or what the price is.
- **Why it fits:** sleeplessness is a real local curse where the weave is thin onto the tapestry that causes it ([[The Veil]], corrected 31 Oct: regional, not world-wide). A cult that sells the cure grows there. Suggestion: it would also find recruits among the overworked of a city that never sleeps. It follows the cult lessons in [[Worldbuilding Sage Lessons#Cults]] and [[Fantasy Forge Lessons#Cults that make sense]]: lonely members, a real fix, and the dark god known only at the top.

**Decided 31 Oct: the god who pays is also the root cause.** The regional sleeplessness comes from this god's tapestry, through **a relic in the elden dwarf hold** of the charming valley (Area 5, [[areas of the Levont]]). The god creates the hunger the Restful sell the cure for. The god is [[#The Kind Hand (decided 31 Oct)|the Kind Hand]].

Still open: what the relic is, what the price is, where the cult is strongest (the charming valley? rail towns on night lines?), and whether the authorities know.

### Birdsong (added 25 Oct)
A cult inside the Larhouse academy of magic in [[Merelon]], gathered by a silver-tongued villain for **the six-eyed raven**. It recruits students who have hit the gods' limits on magic, promising "the sight the gods took away". Whether the raven is one of these gods, a mask for one, or something else is known only at the top.

## Open questions
- How many are there, and what are their names and domains?
- Who first called them that, and do they use the name themselves?
- **How do they wear the accords down?** (Partly answered) The veil thins naturally, but their cultists perform dark rituals to thin it on purpose and bring beings from a tapestry realm through in person ([[The Veil]]). What else do they do?
- What comes through such a ritual: [[Angels]] of their realm, other creatures, something worse? Can it be sent back?
- Do they cooperate, compete, or despise each other?
- **The terms:** (Answered) No god may step into the mortal realm. Gods can grant powers to clerics. Their realms touch the world only where [[The Veil]] grows thin. Who or what enforces the terms?
- Do they have worshippers, cults or churches in the Levont beyond evil factions? What is their legal status (9.2)?
- Were the [[Dragons]] among them?
- Do any of them want to reverse the limits on magic and ascension, and would that make them heroes to some?
