# Northern Elves

The remnants of the elves who joined the betrayer gods in [[The Sundering]]. They live on the [[Northern Continent]]. They have lost the resurrection other elves have, and they aspire to become liches and undead instead. "Northern elves" is a working name.

## What we know
- They sided with the betrayer gods, the elves' name for [[The Gods of Desirenthropy]].
- Their remnants live on the **[[Northern Continent]]**, which is on the map but not yet named.
- **They cannot be resurrected** the way other elves can.
- They **aspire to lichdom and undeath** instead.
- **They worship [[Faelath]], the hunter** of the [[Elven Pantheon]] (29 Oct). All elves worship the hunter, but the others are cautious about invoking her.
- **They lost rebirth too (decided 26 Oct).** Other elves are reborn ([[Death and the Afterlife#Elves are reborn (decided 26 Oct)]]); the northern elves are not.
- **Their necromancy (decided 26 Oct):** they draw the soul out of the body and bind it to a body, much like lichdom in D&D.
- **Body:** taller, slender, with long arms and legs, like the [[Finelor]], but without their horns and bestial faces.
- **Other elves call them the Caillten** (decided 13 Nov 2026): the Finelor and Aelnor name, from Elvish *caillte*. The name means "the lost": lost to their god (they lost rebirth) and lost to the other elves, lost kin (decided 13 Nov 2026). It sounds like *caillen*, "veil", on purpose (an echo).

## The Airden (decided 13 Nov 2026)
- **The northern elves were Airden too,** reworked like the others, and split off later, in [[The Sundering]]. Full summary: [[Aelnor#The Airden (decided 13 Nov 2026)]].
- **The rework is a wound,** above all for them: they already distrust their maker, [[Athréth]].
- **They keep the animal traits all elves keep:** long, large ears like a hunting animal's, pointier noses, narrowed eyes with dark eyeliner-like lines, sharper teeth.
- **Kin trees:** a soul is reborn only near a living kin tree, whose roots reach the Waiting Wood. Kin trees can be corrupted and become anchors for other tapestries, with horrible results.
- **No oaths and the closed night:** elves swear no oaths, and once a year shut their doors to all outsiders.

## The three branches of the elves
| Branch | Side in the Sundering | Body | Death |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[Aelnor]] | Fought beside the good gods | Elven, sun-touched | Can be resurrected |
| [[Finelor]] | Defected from the betrayers to the good side | Tall, long-limbed, horned, bestial faces | Unknown |
| Northern elves | Stayed with the betrayer gods | Tall, long-limbed, no horns, elven faces | Cannot be resurrected or reborn. Bind their souls to bodies by necromancy |

## Reading it together (suggestions, not canon)
- **The long bodies may be the betrayer side's mark.** The Finelor and the northern elves share it, and both were on that side once. Time spent in a betrayer god's tapestry may have stretched them ([[The Veil]]: time in a realm mutates). The Finelor's horns and faces would then come later, from their ordeal of defection.
- **Their undeath is a substitute.** They lost the elves' resurrection, so they reach for the only other way to cheat death. That makes them frightening but understandable, which fits the gray pantheon.
- **Who took their resurrection?** If elven resurrection comes from the [[Elven Pantheon]], the Moon goddess or another elven god may have withdrawn it as punishment.
