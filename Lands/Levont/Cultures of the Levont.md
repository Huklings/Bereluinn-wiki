# Cultures of the Levont

How people across the [[Levont]] live, worship, bury their dead, keep their word and celebrate. The gods themselves are in the [[Pantheon]]. This note is about religion as people practise it.

## Religion in daily life
- **Many churches, uneven standing.** It's open whether all gods are treated alike or the good gods are official and the rest merely tolerated (9.2).
- **Who you turn to:**
    - Birth and healing: [[The Sunturner]]'s healers and midwives.
    - Newborns and orchards: [[The Bud and the Sprout]].
    - Nature and animals: [[Lady Bluecloak]].
    - Duty, hard challenges, or finding something lost: blessings from [[Tapio]]'s clergy and the [[Green Wardens]].

    The first three overlap, and their clergy may compete (9.19).
- **Small signs of faith:** suggestion: a tear-shaped charm for [[The Martyr]], with masks kept to one order (5.1).
- **Art and beauty:** suggestion: [[Lusitri]]'s imagery appears in theatres, craft guilds and certain households, not everywhere (9.16).
- **The returned:** people who met [[The Smiling Father]] and came back. Possibly a small secret fellowship (9.12).
- **Dwarven shrines:** to [[Verdan Ironoath]] (duty), [[Lusitri]] (beauty) and [[Ishgadrum]]. None to death (11.11).
- **Bogeyman gods:** [[The Gods of Desirenthropy]] are known mostly by word of mouth, and the stories have made them darker and crueler than they were. Parents and preachers use them to frighten. Many evil factions align with them.
- **Local cults:** a religious order of bird worshippers trains captains in the marsh county seat ([[areas of the Levont]], Area 4). It isn't tied to any known god yet.
- **Church and state:** the state may use the Martyr's "needs of the many" to justify requisition and conscription (5.2).

## Death and burial
See [[Death and the Afterlife]] for the doctrine.
- **Souls can change hands (2 Nov).** Whoever turns to another pantheon, of any people, can have their soul claimed by those gods in a bargain.
- [[Dwarves]] cremate their dead and keep the ashes in a Dolumar. Ash is mixed at the Ashen Wall.
- The Oathbound, a dwarf clan seldom seen in the Levont, keep their dead in jade name-stones instead and can call them up in battle. Most people know them only through rumor ([[Dwarves#Oathbound (Thrunoz): the jade carriers]]).
- Resurrection is disputed. [[The Bud and the Sprout]] allow only a new body, while [[The Sunturner]] has Rebuke Death (9.18).
- Elven death customs are unknown. How was [[Heleriam Aeloven]] honored? (12.9)

## Oaths, rank and honor
- **Oaths:** [[Verdan Ironoath]]'s faith shapes how people swear. Wartime oaths may still bind officers (9.5).
- **Dwarven allegiance:** no clan swears to one person alone, so kin can end up on opposite sides.
- **Rank:** the [[Order of the Rising Sun]] is a knightly aristocracy. Dwarven clans are also rank-conscious (11.15).
- **Battle honors:** nobles on the ruined-temple coast like to show off battle honors (Area 1).

## Hospitality, contests and guilds
- **Guest-right:** [[The Contest of the 41 Pillars]] ended with arms drawn at a banquet. Who broke hospitality is disputed (12.4).
- **Craft contests:** masonry and smithing challenges may be a recognized way to settle pride (12.8).
- **Guilds:** craft guilds, a famous thieves' guild (Area 5), and possibly cross-border species guilds such as a dwarven mining guild (2.3).

## Peoples
- [[Aelnor]]: proud and adventurous. Wooden apartments in at least one district.
- [[Finelor]]: secluded and stigmatized.
- [[Dwarves]]: clan hierarchy, beard styles by clan, death rites. Secretive (2 Nov): Dwarvish is rarely spoken openly, and only sworn Dwarf-friends are let into dwarven culture ([[Dwarves#A secretive people (decided 2 Nov)]]).
- Humans: their souls are the easiest for any god to win, since they have no church of their own pantheon (2 Nov, [[Death and the Afterlife#Souls can change hands (decided 2 Nov)]]). The great houses are in [[Great Houses of the Levont]].
- [[Halffolk]]: the Wendfolk, in the country and (as Tonfolk) in the cities, hospitality, and the adding-and-subtracting philosophy (28 Oct).
