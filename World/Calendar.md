# Calendar

The common reckoning of days, months and seasons. Shape decided 28 Oct 2026; the names are still drafts. Tidied 29 Oct. For putting it into the Calendarium plugin, see [[Calendar Setup]].

## Decided (28 Oct)
- **A week is a tenday.**
- **A month is three tendays,** 30 days.
- **Twelve months,** so the **four seasons** get three months each.
- **Five festival days** sit between months and belong to no month: **four mark the season changes**, and the fifth is perhaps **New Year**.
- That makes **365 days**, so ages and dates in the [[Timeline]] mean what readers expect.
- **Tied to the world, not the gods.** Months and weeks are named for the land, the sky and the year's work, never for gods.
- **Seasons get descriptive names.** "The harvest months" is the kind of term that works.
- **"Tenday" stays** (29 Oct), even though Forgotten Realms uses it.
- **Two moons remain** (29 Oct), and the broken third circles the world as a debris belt ([[The Moon's Shattering]]).
- **Month names need more than plain descriptions.** Old Norse and Old High German names were tried and didn't land. The two directions you liked: **months named after constellations**, with **invented names in the common tongue**.

## The week and its days (not set)
- **Ten days to a week, called a tenday.** Kept on purpose (29 Oct), even though the word points at the Forgotten Realms (below).
- **Day names: not chosen.** Two directions to pick from:
  - **Sky and land:** sun, moon, stars, field, river and so on, in keeping with the constellation months.
  - **Work:** named for what the day is for, with a market day and a rest day somewhere in the ten.
- Like the months, day names would be invented common-tongue words, never gods' names.

## Festival days (names not set)
- **Four season days,** one at each change of season, between months 3 and 4, 6 and 7, 9 and 10, and 12 and 1.
- **A fifth day,** perhaps New Year. Where it sits is open (see the suggestions).

## Suggestions (not canon)
- **Each people sees its own sky.** Dwarves see the Anvil; elves may see a harp in the same stars, with an older name. Folk calendars come free: the Wendfolk might call Ylsa, the slaughter month, the **Larder-month** ([[Halffolk#Suggestions (not canon)]]).
- **The sky belongs to mortals.** The gods' symbols live in the tapestries, not the stars.
- **Navigation.** Sailors, Wendfolk couriers and Wendfolk guides steer by these stars.
- **The fifth day as a thin day.** A day outside the months when the veil runs thin and folk stay in with candles lit, with New Year on the morning after: survive the night, celebrate the day. It could tie to the broken moon's **moon nights** ([[The Moon's Shattering]]).
- **New Year beside the spring season day,** so the year starts with sowing.
- **A leap day** every four years for the extra quarter day, perhaps another thin day, and an omen.

## Watch out: the Calendar of Harptos
Checked while writing this up: Forgotten Realms' **Calendar of Harptos** is almost the same shape. Twelve 30-day months of three **tendays**, five festival days between months (Midwinter, Greengrass, Midsummer, Highharvestide, the Feast of the Moon), and a leap festival every four years. The structure is real history too (the French Revolutionary calendar used ten-day weeks, 30-day months and five extra days), but the word "tenday" points straight at the Realms. Ways to make it yours:
- ~~**Rename the week.**~~ Decided 29 Oct: "tenday" stays.
- **The constellation months** are already your own; Harptos names months for the weather and the year's work.
- **Avoid a "feast of the moon" fifth day,** or make it clearly about your broken moon.
See [[Borrowed Ideas]].
