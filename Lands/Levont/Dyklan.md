# Dyklan

The Levont's **seventh county**, in **slot C** of the [[Levont Area Map]], the far east. It is the country of **the Giants' Dyke**, a vast wall across the mountain pass between slot C's two ranges, with the fog of [[The Calenwood]] on the far side. Made from the 12 Nov 2026 card draw ([[Card Pull 2026-11-12 - Mountain, martial culture, tavern songs]]); full proposal in [The Giants' Dyke (proposal)](https://claude.ai/artifact/CiftNAA6h9cXuhjZqa4Sz5).

## Decided (12 Nov 2026)
- **Where:** slot C. **The Dyke stands in the mountains, across the gap between slot C's northern and southern ranges.** The Calenwood's fog fills the pass and the coastal strip beyond it. (You chose the slot, then moved the wall into the pass.)
- **The giants built it.** Its core is far larger than anything humans have built, which is how everyone can tell it is giant work. Humans only added to it.
- **Giants still live on the other side,** among old ruins in the forest that now fills it. **Most giants now live on the tapestry side**, in [[The Beast Tapestry]]; some venture out to their old ruins, where Levont people may fight them. **The giants see the Levont folk as trespassers; the Levont folk see the giants as huge, hostile monsters.**
- **The long-lost heir to these lands is a giant,** heir to the old giant country of the ruins, and **the roars are the giants calling for them.**
- **The heir is lost in time:** they went into the tapestry long ago, time runs wrong there, and the giants have been calling for centuries. The heir may not know they are the one being called.
- **A county of its own,** linked to the charming valley (County 5) by road and trade. It was part of County 5 at the founding and **split off later, after a roar year when County 5's count failed to defend the wall.**
- **Names:** the wall is **the Giants' Dyke** (the wall families say "the Dyke"). The mountain over the pass is **the Lavard**, from Elvish *Liavaird*, "high mountain"; locals think it means "lord" and call it **the Old Lord**. The county is **Dyklan**: "the Dykelands", worn down by people who kept shortening it.

## The land (suggestion, not canon)
- **Size:** at the canon scale (about 0.5 km per pixel on the area slots map), the pass is about 25 km across; the fog land behind the Dyke is about 2,500 to 3,000 km² (Luxembourg); all of slot C is about 10,000 km² (Cyprus). Too small to feed a giant nation on its own, which is why most giants live on the tapestry side.
- **The Dyke:** two layers. The giant core across the pass floor, with stones the size of houses. On top and up both mountain shoulders, human banks, towers, watch-cairns and one gate, added over centuries and zigzagging where each family extended its own bit. That human add-on is the "sprawling" part.
- **The wrong-way stretch:** the oldest stretch has its ditch on the Levont side. Suggestion: the beast giants built it to keep the [[Toad People]] out of the wood (they hate the toads, [[Giants]]).
- **The wall country:** farms, hamlets and wall-houses west of the Dyke, in the foothills and the woods at the ranges' western feet. Oats, sheep, goats, a little barley.
- **Peoples:** mostly human; Wendfolk families on the northern stretches (Wendmark is near); dwarves who cut and lay stone; a few elves who remember the wood by its Elvish name.
- **Far away:** about 437 km from the capital. The railroad doesn't come here.

| Oats, sheep, wool | Wall families | Themselves; a little goes out via the charming valley | Barely feeds the strip in a bad year |

| The **wall-penny** | The council | The gates, horns and towers | Inland, people ask why they pay for a wall against monsters they never see |

**A century ago:** before the union the old country of this corner paid for the wall in kind. At the founding the council took it over as a coin levy, the wall-penny, a target whenever the council needs money.

## Who runs it (suggestion, not canon)
1. **The wall families** (stretch-holders). Each household holds a stretch by **stretch-right**: the land behind it, in return for keeping it and turning out when the horns sound. They meet at the **Wall Moot** at the Lavard's foot. A militia culture: every child learns the horn calls, bow and spear; nobody is a knight. The mirror of [[The March]] in the west, where lords hold the passes by the sword.
2. **The [[Calenwood Circle]] and the [[Green Wardens]].** Allies in keeping things in, rivals over the hunting. The druids know the beastfolk are real; they may suspect what the roars mean.
3. **The county's count and the council.** The council pays the wall-penny and wants a say. Who the count is, and how the post is filled, is open.
4. **The wall-houses.** Taverns built into the gate and towers. The songs are sung there, and the songs are the only record of who holds which stretch.

**The other side:** to the council treasurer, the wall-penny pays poor families to frighten people. Most years nothing comes out of the fog that a few hunters can't handle.

## Folklore (suggestion, not canon)
- **Saying:** "Hold your stretch." Do your duty, on the Dyke; mind your own business, inland.
- **Object:** the **name-stone**. A child's name is cut into a stone of the family's stretch at birth.
- **Custom:** the **Long Singing** at midwinter: each wall-house sings its stretches' songs in order along the Dyke, all night. A stretch whose song nobody sings is abandoned.
- **Opening formula:** "Stone by stone and name by name."
- **The heir's song:** "the heir comes back when the roar comes". The families think it promises a human heir; it is a garbled retelling of giant lore (Folklore Lessons 4: variants show who is telling).
- **Who built it?** Wall families: their first holders, stone by stone (validation). Inland folk: giants, as a figure of speech for something huge and old (escape); they don't know it's true. The few elves who know the wood: older than any human claim, and best left alone (control).

## Plot hooks
1. **A roar year.** The roar sounds for the first time in decades. Giants come to the ruins looking for their heir, and the wall families think it's an attack.
2. **The penny and the roar.** The council votes to cut the wall-penny the season the roar returns. A wall family rides to the capital to argue, and nobody believes them.
3. **The heir walks out.** Someone comes out of the fog who doesn't know who they are, and the roaring stops. Who reaches them first: the giants, the druids, or a wall family who thinks the song meant them?
4. **The wrong-way stretch.** An old song describes what the oldest stretch was built to keep out.

## Borrowing check
- **The Wall in *A Song of Ice and Fire*:** close in idea (a wall at the edge, a threat the rest of the realm dismisses). Structure changed: no sworn order or oath, no ice; built by giants and held by farmer families by stretch-right; the "monsters" are a people looking for their heir. To score in [[Borrowed Ideas]].
- **Hadrian's Wall, Offa's Dyke; earthworks blamed on giants or the devil:** real history and folklore, fine.
- **A giant parent roaring for a lost child** echoes monster films such as *Gorgo* (1961); a common trope, and the heir to a realm changes its shape.
- **Avoided:** *Etten-* (Old English for giant) because of Tolkien's Ettenmoors. *Thursdike* is a spare name.
