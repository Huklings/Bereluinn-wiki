# Ádhín

The elven goddess of luck, of chance and of the dared risk, in the [[Elven Pantheon]]. Named 8 Nov 2026.

## The name
Elvish, from Gaelic *ádh*, "luck", plus *-ín*, "beloved": "dear luck", like [[Gaesín]]. Said AW-een (the *dh* is soft, almost silent) ([[Languages and Naming#Elvish (direction 19 Oct, kit approved 22 Oct)]]).

## Decided (8 Nov)
- **Goddess of luck, chance and the dared risk.** Elves hate spending lives, since every elf killed leaves a gap until rebirth ([[Death and the Afterlife#Elves are reborn (decided 26 Oct)]]). They turn to her only when a risk can't be avoided: a crossing, a battle, a birth.
- **True neutral.** She took no side in [[The Sundering]].
- **No tie to [[The Martyr]].** She has no link to the moon or the shards.
- **Light:** the courage to try. **Shadow:** luck doesn't care who deserves it.

## Her tapestry: the Forking Country (decided 8 Nov)
Like every tapestry, it embodies its essence rather than containing it: a realm where chance is everywhere and stronger ([[The Tapestries]]). Luck in the mortal world still falls on its own, and the [[Halffolk]]'s luck still comes from [[Greenhand]].
- **Rule:** nothing falls the same way twice.
- **The realm:** an endless country of roads under a gold-and-violet dusk. Every road forks, and every fork forks again. When the light fails each evening, the land redraws itself: hills change places, rivers find new beds, and yesterday's road leads somewhere else. No map of it has ever been right twice. Waystones at every fork point a different way each time you look.
- **Hazard, the Wandering City:** her city never stands at the same crossroads twice. It is a vast, almost-gambling den of lantern-hung halls where the games never stop. **Luck spills from people's pockets** there like glinting dust when they win, and pools in the gutters when they lose; urchins and old hands sweep it up (your idea, 8 Nov). The stakes are luck, not coin: gamblers wager the luck of their next crossing, fight or harvest. Lose it, and you may walk home on a road that never ends.
- **The Turning House:** her hall at the heart of Hazard. Its doors open onto a different street every time, and she is said to be playing a game inside that has never finished.
- **Other wonders:** the Crossroads of Ten Thousand, a plain where every road meets for one night a year; the Lost Mile, a stretch of road that is only sometimes there.
- **Who lives there:** road-souls (the dead who dared), great magpies as large as eagles that steal spilled luck, and two minor gods ([[Pantheon]], 29 Oct): a three-legged hare nobody catches, and a crossroads witch who reads knucklebones but tells only half.
- **Beside [[Gaesín]]'s Far Road:** his realm is one road that never arrives; hers is every road at once, forking forever. Overlap is fine ([[The Tapestries]]).

### Near its thin spots ([[The Veil]])
- **Runs of luck,** good and bad, that don't break when they should.
- **Nothing repeats:** a loaded die rolls fair, a routine goes wrong, the same road never takes the same time.
- **Magpies gather,** and people count them.
- **Dice dens spring up,** and lords keep trying to ban them.

### Her shadow
Her mantle has bent her toward the next throw. She may answer a prayer once and never again. Her worshippers call that freedom; others call it fickle.

## Suggestions (not canon)
- **Domains (draft):** Luck, Freedom, Trickery, Travel. Alternates: Change.
- **Folklore:** "Luck owes no one," said after a win and a loss. Counting magpies: one for a loss, two for a gain, more for a choice to make (borrows the English "one for sorrow" rhyme). Elves never say her name before a risk; they tap a bone charm, and say it after.
- **Plot hooks:** the den on a thin spot where dice never repeat, so cheats can't win, the council wants it shut and the Larhouse wants to study it; two magpies following a Finelor courier for a week, and the town deciding the omen before the letter is opened; a gambler who lost their luck in Hazard and came back, and now nothing goes right for anyone near them.

## Borrowing check
Lady-luck goddesses are common (Tymora in Forgotten Realms, Fortuna). Fortuna's wheel is avoided on purpose.
