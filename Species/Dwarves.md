# Dwarves

Made by [[Ishgadrum]] from pieces of himself, then enslaved by [[Dragons]]. Now thirteen clans bound by deals and allegiances. This material comes from older drafts and can still change.

## What we know
- **Clans:** thirteen, each called a *drom*, "hall" (see below). Prolonged enslavement gave them "varying attributes".
- **Allegiances:** no clan swears to one person alone. Brothers can end up on opposite sides.
- **Reputation:** "many consider" them obsessed with death and hierarchy. Their greed is blamed on slavery.
- **Death rites:** they cremate their dead and keep the ashes in a Dolumar. Ash is mixed at the hold's ash wall; every hold has one (decided 25 Oct, see [[#Ash walls, and the Great Ash Wall of Ashkeep]]). The Oathbound are the exception: they keep their dead in jade name-stones (see below). The Ashwall dwarves burn their dead in the forges (see below).
- **Shrines:** to [[Verdan Ironoath]] (duty), [[Lusitri]] (beauty) and [[Ishgadrum]].
- **Homes:** the text places them in the mountains.
- **Beards:** styles vary by clan. Suggestion: keep this a small quirk.
- [[Bosurum Veingold]] is a dwarf of note.

## A secretive people (decided 2 Nov)
- **Dwarves keep their culture to themselves.** The [[Levont]] is a unified melting pot, though in truth several smaller countries under one council. The dwarves live inside it without opening up.
- **Dwarvish is rarely spoken openly.** Outsiders hear it seldom, if ever.
- **Only Dwarf-friends are let in.** An outsider is shown the inside of dwarven culture only after being named a Dwarf-friend, and only under oath.
- **Souls can be lost.** A dwarf who turns to another pantheon can have their soul claimed by those gods instead of [[Ishgadrum]] ([[Death and the Afterlife#Souls can change hands (decided 2 Nov)]]).

### What follows (suggestions, not canon)
- **Becoming a Dwarf-friend:** granted by a clan, not by a single dwarf, for a debt of blood or a long service, and sworn at an oath hall such as Thrundrom.
- **Breaking the oath:** a Dwarf-friend who tells what they learned is struck from the clan's memory, a lesser cousin of the Oathbound grinding a name off its stone.
- **Secrecy feeds the rumours.** What the Levont "knows" about dwarves (obsessed with death and hierarchy, greedy) is what outsiders guess when they are kept out.
- **Two names.** Dwarves may use their Levont clan names (Veingold, the Oathbound) in public and keep the Dwarvish ones (Gulmukhoz, Thrunoz) for themselves.
- **A trade in half-knowledge.** Scholars, forgers and swindlers sell "dwarf secrets", most of them false.
- **Borrowing check:** "Dwarf-friend" is a common genre term (Tolkien, Warhammer Fantasy). Safe to use (your call, 2 Nov).

## How dwarves are named (decided 2 Nov)
- **Every dwarf belongs to a clan, but goes by a parent's name.** A dwarf is called by given name, a *-son* or *-daughter* name, and their clan: **Bran Brennansson of Thrunoz**.
- **A title name is earned.** A dwarf who does something worth remembering earns a title name, which replaces the *-son* or *-daughter* name. If Bran's beard caught fire in battle and the goblins ran from the sight, he becomes **Bran Firebeard of Thrunoz**. Titles like *Ironhand* work the same way.
- **The clan is always "of".** The clan comes last, after "of", in its Dwarvish form (Thrunoz) or its Levont form (the Oathbound).

*Fits with what we have:*
- The sound palette has no *e*, so a name like Brennansson is the Levont spelling of a Dwarvish name ([[Languages and Naming#Dwarvish (direction and roots approved, 19 Oct)]]).
- [[Bosurum Veingold]]: by this rule his full name is Bosurum, son of someone, of the Veingold. "Bosurum Veingold" is how Levont clerks write him, clipping the name down to given name plus clan (the registry-shortening rule). Whether that annoys him is open.

*Suggestions (not canon):*
- **Who grants a title?** Suggestion: others give it and the clan confirms it. Nobody names themselves; a dwarf who tries is mocked.
- **Can a title be lost?** Suggestion: an oathbreaker loses the title and goes back to the parent's name, a public shame short of the Oathbound's grinding-off of names.
- **Does it pass on?** Suggestion: no. Bran's children are Bransson and Bransdaughter, not Firebeards. But a famous title can become a nickname for a whole family.

*Open:* is the *-son* or *-daughter* name taken from the father, the mother, or either?

## The Ashwall dwarves (draft, 21 Oct)
Ashwall is a culture, not a clan.

**Burned in the forge.** The Ashwall dwarves cremate their dead in the forges. The ash adds carbon to the iron, so without meaning to, they make steel. Small parts of the dead's spirits linger in the weapons and tools forged there.

**They don't know why it works.** The Ashwall dwarves have no idea the ash is what makes the steel. They only know their metal is better. To them the rite is purely sacred.

**Why non-dwarves rarely carry dwarf weapons and tools.** The reasons can mix and vary by clan:
- **The clans won't sell their ancestors.** A blade might hold someone's grandmother.
- **The blade feels wrong.** The lingering spirits make it feel wrong, or uncooperative, in foreign hands.
- **Grave-robbers.** In dwarf eyes, a non-dwarf carrying one has robbed a grave.

**The derelict holds.** The monsters squatting in the broken holds sit on forges and armouries full of ancestors. Reclaiming a hold means bringing the dead home, not just taking back ground.

**Ancestor law (decided 25 Oct).** The council once ruled that a dwarf blade is **human remains, not property**. Carrying one is grave-robbing in law. Nobody enforces it: the marcher family's heirloom blades on the wall ([[The March]], hook 3) are a crime on the books. A dwarf who wants a blade back can point to the law, and a lord who wants to keep one can point to a hundred years of nobody caring. Still open: when and why the council made the ruling, and whether any court has ever upheld it.

**Two answers to keeping the dead.** The Ashwall keep theirs in steel, the Oathbound in jade. One does it without knowing, the other on purpose.

**Hooks**
- A dwarf weapon turns up in a human's or a hobgoblin's hands. Whose ancestor is it, and how did it leave the hold? (Compare the Ironhearth hook: the horde is using their forges.)
- Suggestion: a human smith works out that ash makes better steel and starts experimenting with bone ash. To the dwarves that would be deeply offensive.

**Open questions**
- Is Ashwall named for the Ashen Wall? How does burning in the forge fit with keeping ashes in a Dolumar: does some ash go to the Dolumar and the Wall, and the rest into the steel?
- Which clans keep the Ashwall ways? Is there a link to the Ashkeep (*Doloz*, "ash folk"), who burned the dead for the dragons?

## The thirteen clans (draft, 19 Oct)
The word for clan is ***drom***, "hall" (settled 19 Oct). It means both a clan and the hall it lives in, the way "house" does for a noble family. "The Veingold drom" is the Veingold clan.

Each clan's name comes from the work its dragon forced on it. That lore is forgotten now: to dwarves today the names are just names. A clan holds no special role in society because of its name, and every clan has its smiths, priests, merchants and miners.

### Who still knows
The meanings sit in the forgotten layer of history ([[Fantasy Forge Lessons]]). Elves remember further back than dwarves, so an elven scholar might know what the names meant, or [[Lurian]]'s archives might (9.13).

**A hook:** *Gorumoz* meant "overseer folk". If that came out, the Hardhand would face a stigma for something none of them remember doing.

### Hidden holds (decided 9 Nov)
The dwarves may sit in the Levont's council, but they are still a secluded people ([[#A secretive people (decided 2 Nov)]]). Outside the Levont, that seclusion is built into the land.
- **Who feeds it?** A mountain hold can't feed itself ([[#Who feeds the holds (decided 25 Oct)]]). In the [[The Veiled Frontier|Veiled Frontier]] there is no council and no railroad, only petty dukes, so a living hold needs a duke's grain.
- **The Low Hall.** A hidden hold never shows its real door. It trades only at a **Low Hall**, a fortified market at the foot of its mountain, which outsiders often take for the hold itself.
- **The bread oath.** One ducal house feeds the hold, sworn to a **bread oath**: grain, and silence about the door, in return for armour, jade or tools.
- **Three kinds of Frontier hold:** *open* (its gate known), *hidden* (Low Hall and bread oath), and *lost or abandoned*.
- **Limits and costs:** the secret lives in one human family. A succession fight, a loose-lipped heir, or a duke who falls to a neighbour can expose the door. The oath is a leash both ways: if the duke's harvest fails, or he decides to squeeze them, the hold goes hungry.

*Suggestions (not canon):*
- **The other side:** dukes without a hold of their own call the bread-oath houses "dwarf-kept", bought men. Some Levont dwarves call hiding cowardice dressed as wisdom, the old quarrel over the clans who walked away in the Sundering.
- **Folklore:** Frontier children are told *"Never follow a dwarf home"*: a dwarf who's followed leads you into the mountain forever. Grown-ups know the truer version: the duke's men find you first. At a Low Hall a newcomer's first trade is silent, goods left on a stone and taken or not (real-world "silent trade").
- **Hooks:** a dying duke's two heirs both know where the door is, and one plans to sell it; a bad harvest stops the bread, and dwarves seen buying grain at open markets give the hold away; the party escorts a grain train and is told never to look up the valley.
- **Borrowing check:** dwarves trading with a human town at a mountain's foot recalls Erebor and Dale (Tolkien). Here the town doesn't know where the hold is, the bond is a secret oath with one family, and grain is the leash. Low risk.
- **Still suggestion:** Shieldbrow's hold *Gad-Ozgar* in the central range with a Low Hall on each flank; Cragwatch's hidden watch-halls; which other holds are hidden.

### Abandoned in the Sundering (decided 25 Oct)
**Opalhand, Stonehewn and Hardhand** didn't lose their holds to an enemy. They left them during [[The Sundering]], about 600 years ago, rather than be attacked and lose their people. Better a living clan than a full ash wall.
- **Half-remembered:** the stories survive, the roads to the holds don't. Each clan knows it *had* a hold, and roughly where. Nobody has been back, and the exact places are lost.
- **What's still inside:** they left in a hurry, so the ash walls, forges and treasures may still be there. Whoever finds an abandoned hold first, dwarf or not, finds a clan's dead.
- **They care; they are too few (decided 9 Nov).** The dwarves want their lost and abandoned holds back. What stops them is numbers: the clans are too few and too short of hands to retake and staff them, not indifferent.
- **A different shame:** these clans didn't fall, they chose to go. Some dwarves call that wisdom, others cowardice. The exiles who fought and lost (Ironhearth, Ashkeep) don't always welcome the ones who walked away.

### Broadback: taken by a duke (decided 25 Oct)
The Broadback's hold fell recently, within living memory, to one of the Veiled Frontier's petty dukes.
- **Why it fell:** the clan was already diminished. Their hauling trade had drawn many of the young over the mountains into the Levont, and the hold was left short of hands. The duke saw it, and overwhelmed what was left.
- **The grudge:** Broadback who had already gone to the Levont, and those who fled the fall, now share one enemy, the duke who holds their hall. Every Broadback porter on the passes knows his name.
- **Guilt:** the ones who left before the fall carry a second weight. Their going is part of why it fell.
- **Shieldbrow armour (decided 25 Oct):** the duke's soldiers wore Shieldbrow armour when they took the hold. The Shieldbrow sold it to his house long before, a generation or more earlier, in the ordinary way of their trade with every petty duke. To the Broadback that doesn't matter. Dwarf-made armour was used to take a dwarf hold, and they hold the Shieldbrow responsible. The Shieldbrow answer that they sell to everyone, and what a buyer does later is his own affair. A grudge between dwarves, on top of the grudge against the duke.
- **Suggestion (not canon):** Broadback porters refuse to carry anything bound for the duke's lands or the Shieldbrow hold, and charge double for anything coming out.
- Still open: the duke's name, what he does with a dwarf hold, and how long ago it fell.

### Who feeds the holds (decided 25 Oct)
A mountain hold can't feed itself ([[Grainbound Lessons]]: who feeds it?).
- **The standing holds buy their grain** from the human counties, carried by road and rail. Even the Veingold depend on the Levont's harvests and on [[The Railroad]]. A bad harvest or a rail strike hits them as hard as anyone.
- **What they trade for it:** the Veingold pay in coin, the Emberbeard in coal and furnace work, the Salthollow in salt. Salt for grain is the oldest bargain in the Levont.
- **The clans without holds live in Levont towns,** as smiths, masons, haulers and lens-grinders, renting homes from humans. This answers 11.5: yes, dwarves live in towns, river cities and farm country, mostly because they have nowhere else.

### Ash walls, and the Great Ash Wall of Ashkeep
- **Every hold has its own ash wall,** where the clan's ash is mixed and kept beside the Dolumar. The ash wall is the heart of a hold, which is why losing a hold means losing your dead.
- **Ashkeep had the largest of them all,** the great ash wall, where clans once sent their most honoured dead. It's the reason the Ashkeep are called the ash folk.
- **Ashkeep is lost, and never properly held.** It lies near [[Lorweir]], on the old under-mountain roads that run from the Ironhearth hold toward the Levont. Whoever holds the road wants Ashkeep, so the hold changes hands in the dark: horde raiders, monsters, and dwarf expeditions sent to take it back. Skirmishes underground never stop.
- **Why it matters:** retaking Ashkeep means bringing home generations of the honoured dead of many clans, not only the Ashkeep's. It's the cause the [[Kharundrom]] saves for, and a cause the Veingold can't afford to be seen ignoring.
- **The road goes on.** If the under-roads run from Ironhearth through Ashkeep toward the Levont, they may come out at the Deepdelve hold under the March. That would make the March's "road beneath" the end of a road from the horde's own forges.

## The great clans (draft, 19 Oct)
Four clans developed in detail. The rest stay one line each for now.

### Veingold (Gulmukhoz): the power inside the Levont
- **Balanced by the council (25 Oct):** their lease on the underground right runs for a fixed term, the council has the railroad as a second purse, and the Veingold need the council's protection. See [[Government of the Levont#The council and the Veingold (decided 25 Oct)]].
- **Where:** the largest working hold inside the Levont, in the upland mining country.
- **Power:** money. They lend to counties, guilds and other clans, and have friends on the council.
- **Shadow:** much of their power is debt other clans owe them, including exiled clans saving to go home. The elves remember [[The Contest of the 41 Pillars]] as the day a Veingold broke guest-right. The Veingold tell it differently.
- [[Bosurum Veingold]] is one of theirs.

### Ironhearth (Zhurbrakhoz): the lost hold
- **Where:** their great hold stood in the mountains of [[Lorweir]], part of the old empire, Ankhor-Durm. The hobgoblin horde took it.
- **Now:** scattered through Levont towns as some of the finest smiths in the land, with no home to go back to.
- **Want:** to retake the hold. That needs Veingold money, which comes with strings, and Levont soldiers, which the council won't send because it risks provoking the horde.
- **Hook:** hobgoblin blades turn up stamped with Ironhearth marks. The horde is using their forges.

### Shieldbrow (Gadromoz): the hold that never fell
- **Where:** a working hold in the Veiled Frontier. Place it once the map exists.
- **A hotzone (25 Oct):** it still stands, but it's under attack all the time. Raiders, petty dukes' wars and monsters keep coming at its gates. Never fallen, never at peace.
- **Power:** they never joined the Levont. They sell armor to every petty kingdom and "duke" on the Frontier, which makes them quiet kingmakers there.
- **Shadow:** they think Levont dwarves have gone soft among humans and elves. When Ironhearth asks for help, the answer is "pay first."
- **The Broadback grudge (25 Oct):** armour they sold long ago to a Frontier duke's house was worn when he took the Broadback hold. The Broadback blame them for it ([[#Broadback: taken by a duke (decided 25 Oct)]]).

### Oathbound (Thrunoz): the jade carriers
- **Where (placed 9 Nov):** the far south coast range of [[The Veiled Frontier]], far from the Levont. Suggestion: the holds sit on the only known jade, which explains why it's scarce and why they stay. (The other option, near the [[Northern Elves]], is dropped.)
- **In the Levont:** seldom seen. Maybe an envoy now and then, or a lone wanderer with a single stone on their back. Rumors about them are everywhere.

**Name-stones.** Instead of the Dolumar and the Ashen Wall, they carry large jade-inlaid stones called *nuzhar* ("jade vessel"), carved with the names of their dead. Through the stones they channel their ancestors' spirits as power.

**Heresy or veneration (19 Oct).** Other dwarves see the Oathbound as heretics. Dwarf souls should return to the halls of their gods, and the Oathbound trap them in stone instead. The Oathbound see it the other way round: keeping their ancestors with them is the deeper respect, and letting them go would be abandoning them.

**War-stones.** In battle they sometimes carry larger stones, *nuzhdrom* ("jade hall", draft name), and call their ancestors out by the score: armored, armed spirits, jade-colored, bound to fight. Each war-stone takes about four dwarves to carry, and someone able to channel the magic to bring the spirits out. Other dwarves call it necromancy. It lets a small, distant clan stand against far larger factions.
- **The irony:** a people freed from dragon slavery now enslaves its own dead. The Oathbound would say their dead serve willingly, by oath. Who is right?

**Limits**
- **Weight:** name-stones are heavy, and a warrior carries one or two. Each war-stone needs about four bearers plus a channeler, so they're slow and few, and a clan can't field many. Kill the channeler and the stone goes quiet.
- **Spirits fade:** every use spends some of a spirit. A war-stone emptied in one battle can mean a whole line of ancestors gone.
- **Spirits have wills:** they argue, refuse and make demands of the living.
- **Jade is scarce:** not every dead Oathbound gets a stone, and who does is a fight within the clan.
- **Loss:** a stolen or broken stone is a lost ancestor.

**What the Levont says about them** (the truth column is for you, not for players)

| Rumor | Truth |
| --- | --- |
| "They talk to their dead." | True. The *nuzhar* hold their ancestors' spirits |
| "Every one of them carries his grandfather on his back." | Close. The stones are heavy, and a warrior carries one or two |
| "Their dead never rest. That's why they hide." | Half true. Other dwarves call them heretics for keeping souls from their gods' halls, and the Oathbound keep to themselves partly for that reason |
| "In war, a ghost army marches with them." | True. The war-stones |
| "An oathbreaker gets his name ground off the stone." | True, and it's their worst punishment: erased from the clan, alive and dead. Compare the open question of who is excluded from the Ashen Wall (11.9) |
| "They steal the dead of other clans." | False, as far as anyone knows. Undecided |
| "They were never slaves to the dragons." | Unknown. That lore is forgotten |

**Hooks**
- A *nuzhar* turns up in a Levont pawnshop. Someone's ancestor has been sold, and the Oathbound will come looking for it.
- The restless spirit locked in the old dwarven hold in Area 5 ([[areas of the Levont]]) could be a broken or abandoned stone. That would explain the insomnia nearby.
- **PF2e:** the Animist class, which works with spirits, fits an Oathbound character.

**Borrowing check:** an army of armored dead, bound by oath and glowing green, echoes Tolkien's Dead Men of Dunharrow, especially as the films show them. The name "Oathbound" makes the echo louder. The structure differs: these are ancestors kept out of duty and love, not traitors cursed for breaking an oath. Scored in [[Borrowed Ideas]].

## Giants, dragons and the channels (26 Oct)
- **The dwarves have fought the [[Giants]]**, many of whom live in the mountains.
- **The [[Dragons]] most likely fought the giants too, using their enslaved dwarf armies.** *Suggestion:* proud dwarves may retell it as their own war and leave the chains out.
- **The dwarves dug [[The Channels]]** along the elven rivers during the ascension war, without asking the elves. The elves still seethe over it.
- **The elves may come for the Oathbound.** Souls can be trapped, and elves may want to free trapped souls ([[Death and the Afterlife#Souls can be trapped (26 Oct)]]). To an elf, a jade name-stone, and maybe an Ashwall blade, could look like a cage. That stacks a second grievance on top of the channels.
