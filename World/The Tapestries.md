# The Tapestries

The realms of the gods. Each god's tapestry was created when they took their mantle, and they withdrew into it, because staying on the mortal plane was dangerous for everyone else.

## What we know
- **Walled off by [[The Martyr]].** She chose to die, and her shattering walled the tapestry realms off from the mortal realm.
- **The terms:** no god may step into the mortal realm. Gods can grant powers to clerics. A realm's power touches the world only where [[The Veil]] grows thin.
- Spending time inside a realm mutates any being. [[Angels]] are souls changed this way.
- The losers of the Sundering received tapestries too, as part of the terms they had to accept ([[The Gods of Desirenthropy]]).
- The tapestry plan split the wizards and was at the heart of [[The Sundering]]. Its opponents saw it as unfair: it limited their power and took power away from those who had not reached it yet. See [[Sides of the Sundering]].
- Each god has their own tapestry.
- The gods made them by choice, to protect the world from their own presence.
- [[areas of the Levont]], Area 5: a restless spirit in an elden dwarven hold is connected to "the tapestries of domination and greed" and to "the primal tapestries". Its influence is felt "whenever the veil becomes too thin".

## How they were made (decided 26 Oct)
The mantle ritual made them. The side that wanted to rein in the elden wizard-lords invented it: it bound the world's loose magic to each lord as a mantle and created that lord's tapestry. Holding a mantle now gives domain over a tapestry. [[The Martyr]]'s death, while making her own mantle, walled the tapestries off from the mortal realm, and her own tapestry only half works. Full story: [[Pantheon#Where the gods came from (decided 26 Oct)]].

**Why they're hard to pin down (decided 26 Oct):** each tapestry is intimately tied to its lord's power as it was when it was made, and that power permeates the whole realm. [[The Beast Tapestry]] is not only beasts, but the wilds, nature, change, chaos and so on.

## The gods' own realms (26 Oct)
- **Decided:** the mortal realm is **the loom the tapestries hang from**.
- **Decided:** each tapestry is the private realm of one of the wizards who became gods, and it is where people now pull their powers from. So every tapestry is tied to its god and to that god's domains.
- **"The wizards of old"** is the name for all the gods together, not one god's title.
- **Decided:** their ascension is why magic is restricted, and the restriction is built into the shape of the world ([[Magic#How magic is restricted (decided 26 Oct)]]).
- **The feel you're after:** vague but not vague realms, where weird things happen. Still at the cusp of your mind; to spin on further.

### Working ideas from 26 Oct (not settled)
- **A tapestry follows its maker's obsessions,** the way a dream follows the dreamer. The domains are the threads the god chose to weave in, and clerics pull on those threads.
- **Three anchors per tapestry.** One **rule** (time runs backwards, names don't stick, the dead can speak), one **image** nobody forgets, and one **thing that lives there**. Everything else stays unexplained, which is where the strangeness comes from.
- **Owners, histories and wounds,** like the warrens of the Malazan books, where some realms belong to peoples, some were shattered, and their fragments can be claimed. A draft along those lines: a first, crude weaving (the beast tapestry); an elven tapestry where souls wait for rebirth; a dwarven tapestry damaged when the empire fell; a torn tapestry tied to the giants, its scraps hanging over mountain seams; a young human weave stitched from stolen threads; and the Fray, the unwoven gaps where tears lead. If any of this is adopted, score it against Malazan in [[Borrowed Ideas]].
- ~~**The Martyr as the thread?**~~ Superseded 26 Oct: the mantles made the tapestries, and the Martyr has a broken tapestry of her own.
- **What came before the weaving?** [[Giants]] walk the seams and [[Dragons]] are ancient. Did the wizards learn to weave from older powers, or steal the loom from them? Either answer gives giants and dragons a personal reason to resent the gods.

**Gods overlap (decided 31 Oct).** Domains and themes align like a Venn diagram: two gods can both have forests, growth or nature. What differs is the weighting and the rule of each tapestry.

**Thin spots show the realm's own rule (decided 29 Oct).** A thin spot does not cause its own kind of sleeplessness; that idea from 26 Oct is dropped, because the theme was repeating. Instead the realm's rule leaks through, so every thin spot looks different.

**Suggestion (not canon): a mantle bends its holder.** Like a Shard's Intent in Sanderson's Cosmere, holding a mantle slowly turns a lord into its essence. That gives every god a built-in shadow (first used for [[Athréth]]).

## Reading the existing lore through this (suggestions, not canon)
- **Domination and greed** belong to [[The Gods of Desirenthropy]], the losers of the war, who were given tapestries of their own as part of the terms.
- **The primal tapestries** could belong to the nature gods: [[Lady Bluecloak]], [[Tapio]] or [[The Bud and the Sprout]].
- **Souls** go to "whatever god has claimed them" ([[Death and the Afterlife]]). That would mean into that god's tapestry.
- **The Martyr's binding:** the Martyr's myth says the weakest god bound the others to their domains. The official story says the gods withdrew freely. A world where both stories exist has a built-in religious dispute.
