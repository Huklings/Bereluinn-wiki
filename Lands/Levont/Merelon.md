# Merelon

A city on the southeast coast and its islands, built over the ruins of an elden elven city of magic. Today it is home to one of the Levont's licensed schools of magic (until 12 Nov called the **Larhouse**; new name to come), and the old stones of the fallen city stand inside its walls, disguised as rubble. Area 7, from an old draft (added 25 Oct 2026).

## The name
- **Muirenchloe**, "sea stones" (*muiren* "sea" + *cloe* "stone", softened to *chloe*): the elves' name for the old city, after the standing stones above the sea ([[Languages and Naming#Elvish (direction 19 Oct, kit approved 22 Oct)]]).
- **Merelon** is what humans made of it: *Muirenchloe* worn down to *Mer'n-lo*, then Merelon. Almost nobody in the city knows it means "sea stones", which is useful, because the stones are the city's real secret.
- Aelnor elders still say *Muirenchloe*, and say it pointedly ([[Rivers of the Levont#Names]]).

## Where
**Slot B** (moved 12 Nov from slot C): the southeast coast and its islands, facing Trelmheir, beside the salt steppe (Area 3) ([[Levont Area Map]]). About 367 km from the capital, one of the farthest places in the Levont, which gives the region prestige; the council is happy to keep magic a long way off. The Calenwood stayed behind in slot C ([[The Calenwood]]).

### The siege and the salt (decided 12 Nov)
- **The elven city was attacked in the Sundering.** The salt steppe next door, whose earth "the war of elden eras" salted ([[areas of the Levont]], Area 3), is the battlefield where its elves fought and died.
- **The islands off the coast** hold more of the old elven city and its kingdom.
- **The toads live on Trelmheir** (decided 12 Nov), so Merelon faces the front line ([[Toad People]]).

**Suggestions (not canon):**
- *Front-line magic:* the council tolerates a school of casters this far out partly because they guard the coast against the toads.
- *Folklore:* where the elves fell, saplings of the Waiting Wood try to sprout every spring, and the salt kills them ([[Athréth]]). Steppe folk call them "the spring dead".
- *Hooks:* a student finds an elven name in a salt crust that wasn't there yesterday; toad raiders land on an island ruin the school has never surveyed; an Aelnor family asks the school to escort them to the battlefield to mourn.

## The fallen city
- **Muirenchloe** was a city of elven magic in [[The Elden Era]], when magic was still at full strength. It fell around the time of [[The Sundering]], when the gods broke magic ([[Magic]]).
- **The standing stones** survive inside the Larhouse grounds, passed off as ancient rubble. They are an elden relic: whatever they were built to do, they still do some of it. They are the real reason the school was built here.
- **Who knows:** the Larhouse masters, a few Aelnor families, and [[Lurian]]'s archive houses. Students are told the stones are "foundations".

## The Larhouse
**Changed 12 Nov:** the name *Larhouse* is dropped (a new name is to come), and this is **one of at least two licensed schools** in the Levont, founded by [[Wizard Houses]]. The old name was the common tongue's *lār-hūs*, "lore-house"; this note still says "Larhouse" until the new name is chosen.
- **A licensed school** of arcane magic. It answers 14.6 in part: this is where casters are trained.
- **Graduates owe service:** years as council casters, certifying thin spots for reeves, fitting shard furnaces for [[The Railroad]], or keeping the [[Moon Shards]] registers.
- **Why here:** the stones. Magic is a little easier in their shadow, and a little more dangerous.
- **Limits** (working idea): if heavy magic thins [[The Veil]], a school full of casters on an elden site is a thin spot waiting to happen. The Calenwood and its druids used to be the safeguard.
- **What keeps the thin spots in check (decided 12 Nov):** the local count, in a combined effort between the count's forces and the mages and wizards the tower produces. The tower and its creed are forced to work magic only in limited amounts, so as not to create more thin spots. Keeping within those limits may even be one of the tower's trials.
  - *Suggestions (not canon):* the trial could be a student holding a thin spot steady for a night without widening it, under the count's soldiers' eyes. Folklore: "The count holds the spear, the tower holds its breath." Hook: the count's line and the tower quarrel over who decides when a thin spot is "too thin", and a village waits between them.

## The Calenwood (moved out, 12 Nov)
The Calenwood and its druids stayed in slot C when Merelon moved. Everything about them is now in [[The Calenwood]] and [[Calenwood Circle]].

## Hidden: Birdsong (not for players)
A silver-tongued villain has joined the Larhouse, as a master or a well-liked senior student, and is quietly gathering followers for **the six-eyed raven**. The cult calls itself **Birdsong**.
- **Who it recruits:** students who have hit the gods' limits on [[Magic]]. The raven promises "the sight the gods took away", the power the Sundering removed. That is the losers' argument from [[Sides of the Sundering]], offered to frustrated young casters.
- **What it really is:** the six-eyed raven is the face the members see. Whether it is a god of desirenthropy, a mask for one, or a being from behind the veil is known only at the top ([[The Gods of Desirenthropy#Cults]]).
- **The bird link:** the marsh county's religious order of bird worshippers trains captains with red-winged crows ([[areas of the Levont]], Area 4). Birdsong could be hiding inside that order, or imitating it. Either way the honest bird order will be blamed when Birdsong is found.
- **What it wants:** the stones. A school full of casters around an elden relic is exactly what a cult needs to thin the veil on purpose.
