# Kharundrom

A savings society of exiled dwarf clans, pooling money to retake their lost holds without Veingold loans. Created 22 Oct 2026 from a mock-up. **Only the name and the core idea are decided; the rest is suggestion.**

## The name (decided 22 Oct)
*Kharundrom*: *khar* "burden" + *un* "old" + *drom* "hall". **"The burden of the old halls."** A heavy, bitter name for exiles who carry the halls they lost every day ([[Languages and Naming#Dwarvish (direction and roots approved, 19 Oct)]]).

## What it is
- **Members:** exiled clans and families whose holds fell, above all the Ironhearth (*Zhurbrakhoz*), whose great hold in [[Lorweir]] the horde took ([[Dwarves]]). Since 25 Oct, most clans have lost their holds: Ashkeep, Opalhand, Broadback, Deepdelve, Hardhand and the hold-less Stonehewn are all potential members ([[Dwarves#The holds (decided 25 Oct)]]).
- **The great cause:** retaking Ashkeep and its great ash wall, on the contested under-road between Ironhearth and the Levont, would bring home the honoured dead of many clans.
- **Purpose:** to fund the day they go home: expeditions, mercenaries, arms and the bringing-home of their dead from the broken forges.
- **Why it exists:** the Ironhearth need money, and the only lender is the Veingold, whose loans come with strings. The Kharundrom is the exiles' answer: their own coin, owed to no one.

## Suggestions (not canon)
- **The iron vessel.** Each member family keeps a sealed iron box, a *zhurumar* (*zhur* "iron" + *-umar* "vessel"), and drops in one coin a week. It stays sealed until the day of return. Opening one early is a disgrace.
- **Shadow.** It smuggles ancestor-steel back from the derelict holds, and it funds raids the council never approved. The council fears it could drag the Levont into a war with the horde.
- **Rivals.** The Veingold see it as a threat to their hold on the exiles' debts. The Shieldbrow think it's a waste: "pay first."
- **Believable side.** To its members it is love and duty: the dead in the lost forges are their grandparents.
