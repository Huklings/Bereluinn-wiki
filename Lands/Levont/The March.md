# The March

The mountain range on the [[Levont]]'s western edge, between the Levont and the larger continent of [[The Veiled Frontier]] and [[Adaria]]. A line of castles and tower keeps, each with its villages, guards the way in. "The March" is a working name.

## Decided (22 Oct)
- **Where:** on the map draft, the range runs along the Levont's western edge, from the northern bay down to the coast where the southern peninsulas begin. It covers the whole land route in from the west.
- **What:** a series of castle and tower keeps, each with its villages, guarding the entrance to the Levont.
- **Terrain:** small mountain passes, and forests that make the passes harder to cover.
- **Model: the Welsh Marches.** Chosen because it gives friction while staying grounded in a real situation.
- **The ruin beneath:** at least one keep stands over an old, derelict dwarf hold. The hold is a second way in for threats, one the walls above don't cover.

## Northern and southern marches (22 Oct)
**Decided:** the March splits in two. The [[Order of the Rising Sun]] holds the **northern marches**, from the rocky coast down to just north of the main gate. **It does not hold the main gate**, which lies in the southern marches.

**The Order's sallies (decided 22 Oct):** the Order resents not holding the gate. When the main gate or another southern pass is attacked, it rides south to "help", invited or not, then bills the defenders for its expertise and skill. The southern lords call it extortion. See [[Order of the Rising Sun]] for hooks.

**Suggestion:** the old border kingdom was the Levont's original march. At the founding, its southern stretch, gate and all, went to new lords, and the Order kept the north. Both sides think the wall is theirs.

**Open:** within the southern marches, who holds the main gate itself: one marcher lord, several in turn, or a council garrison?

## The real model: the Welsh Marches
After 1066, Norman lords were set along the Welsh border to hold it by their own swords. In return they got rights no ordinary lord had:
- They could build castles and wage war without the king's licence.
- They ran their own courts. The king's writ did not run in the March.
- Their lands were their own conquests, held "by the sword", not granted piece by piece.

Kings needed them and distrusted them. Marcher families such as the Mortimers grew powerful enough to unseat a king. The special status lasted until the Laws in Wales Acts (1536–1543) folded the March into the ordinary shires.

**Why it fits the Levont:** the counties already hold the real power and the council referees ([[Government of the Levont]]). Marcher lords are a sharper version of that: the people guarding the door know they can't easily be replaced.

## How the defence works (suggestion, not canon)
- **Gate-keeps** at the one or two main passes: real castles, with walls across the valley floor. On the map draft, the obvious main gate is about halfway down the range, where a forest patch and a spur of peaks push east into the Levont.
- **Towers** above the minor passes. They can't stop an army, but they can see one.
- **A beacon chain** of signal fires linking the towers back into the heartland (the Byzantines ran one for real).
- **Woodwardens or rangers** for the forested stretches, where walls can't go. The southern end of the range is forested down to the coast. That wood could be one of the [[Preserves and Parks]], named for whoever holds it, such as "the Margrave's Wood". Then poaching and trespass become border-security problems too, and the [[Green Wardens]] have a stake.

**Who they face:** the big landmass to the west leads to the Veiled Frontier's petty dukes in one direction and [[Lorweir]]'s hobgoblin horde in the other. The march faces raids and politics from the southwest, and the threat of the horde from the northwest.

## Plot hooks
1. **The road beneath.** The derelict hold under one keep is a second door into the Levont. It was the **Deepdelve** hold, and the under-roads behind it may run all the way to Ashkeep and the horde's Ironhearth forges ([[Dwarves#The holds (decided 25 Oct)]], 25 Oct). What comes up from below? Does the marcher lord know, and if so, is he keeping it quiet, sealing it, or quietly selling passage through it?
2. **Whose hold is it?** A dwarf clan claims the ruin under the keep. Reclaiming it means bringing their dead home from its forges and armouries ([[Dwarves]], the Ashwall dwarves). The marcher lord sits on top of it and has no wish to share his foundations.
3. **Ancestor steel on the wall.** The marcher family displays dwarf weapons dug out of the ruin as heirlooms. To a dwarf, those blades hold someone's ancestors, and the family are grave-robbers. In law they are too: under ancestor law a dwarf blade is remains, not property (decided 25 Oct, [[Dwarves]]). Nobody has ever enforced it.
4. **A keep goes silent.** Its beacon doesn't answer. Has it been taken, bribed, or has its lord declared himself independent?
5. **The council's writ.** The council wants its own officers in the march: way stations, reeves, its own law (14.18). The marcher lords answer that the council's writ does not run here, and they have the swords to back it.
6. **A private war.** A marcher lord uses his right to make war on a Veiled Frontier duke over a raid. If it goes badly, the whole Levont is dragged in, and the council never agreed to any of it.
7. **Divided loyalties.** A marcher house has married into a Veiled Frontier ducal family, as the Mortimers married into Welsh princely lines. Its heir has cousins on both sides of the passes.
8. **Smugglers in the wood.** Goods slip through the forest paths to avoid the gate tolls. If [[The Indigo Outpost]] sits on the western coast, its dye cakes could be part of that trade.
9. **Refuge for outlaws.** Where the council's law doesn't reach, fugitives go. A marcher lord who takes them in gains swords and makes enemies in the heartland.

## Name ideas
The Levont's humans use Anglo-Saxon and Germanic roots ([[Languages and Naming]]), so "march" fits: Old English *mearc*, German *Mark*.
- *The Westmark*
- *Wealdmearc*, "forest-march"
- *The Ashmark*, if Ashwall dwarves are involved
- A lord there would be a **margrave** or a **marcher lord**.

## Open questions
- A thin line runs down through the range on the map draft, separate from the mountains. Is it a border, a river, or a narrow strait of sea? If it's water, the keeps guard a crossing as well as passes.
- How many keeps, and how many main passes?
- Are the southern marcher lords part of a county, a county of their own, or outside the six counties altogether?
- Who holds the main gate within the southern marches: one lord, several, or a council garrison? (Not the Order, decided 22 Oct.)
- Do the southern lords ever pay the Order's bills, and who decides what's fair?
- Did their rights come from the founding, from the war, or from before the union?
- Which keep stands on the dwarf ruin, and what lives in the ruin now?
- What is the march called?
