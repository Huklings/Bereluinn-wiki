# Rivers of the Levont

The Levont's rivers, drawn on 22 October 2026 from the map draft. **The rivers and their names were approved on 22 Oct.** The consequences below are marked where they're still suggestions. Rivers come before borders, forests and towns ([[Grainbound Lessons]], [[Fantasy Forge Lessons]]), so once these are settled, most other placements follow from them.

![[Levont rivers suggestion.png]]

## Feasibility fixes (decided 24 Oct)
A feasibility study checked every river against how real rivers behave ([Levont rivers: feasibility study](https://claude.ai/artifact/5mokNhpeUHP52TGf1gHgDK)). Four changes follow, shown on the drawing guide below:
1. **A ridge south of the Mallon.** A line of hills from the March to the slot A mountains, like the Downs south of the Thames. Without it, water from the main gate would drain into the southwest inlets, only 35 to 50 km away.
2. **The confluence moves inland.** The Mallon and the Dovan meet in a Y about 45 km inland, and one river flows on south into the estuary, as the Garonne and Dordogne do in the Gironde.
3. **The salt valley is a closed, dry basin.** See point 6 below.
4. **Hills along the south edge of the charming valley,** so the Aislan drains north to the northeast coast, not south.

![[Levont rivers - drawing guide.png]]

## How they were placed
- **Every river starts in high ground** (the March, the crook, the Veingold uplands, the eastern range) **and takes the shortest downhill way to the sea.**
- **Rivers join on the way down and never split,** except in a delta at the mouth.
- **A main divide** runs west to east across the middle of the Levont, from the March to the Veingold uplands. North of it, water runs to the north coast. South of it, water runs to the big V-shaped bay on the south coast.
- Lengths below are measured on the map at the canon scale ([[World Scale]]), with about a third added for meanders.

## What follows from them
**1. The estuary will be the Levont's great trading city.** *(Suggestion.)*
An estuary is the wide, tidal mouth of a river, where fresh water meets the sea. Ships can come in on the tide, and river boats can come down to meet them.
The Plains River and the Basin River join about 45 km inland and flow on as one river into the same bay (marked 8 on the map; confluence moved 24 Oct). That makes it a junction in every sense the Grainbound uses: road meets river, river meets river, river meets sea. In a real medieval country this is where the biggest market town grows, and it could rival the capital in wealth.
- **The river-trade country** from [[Former Countries of the Levont]], the one with the Old Norse skin and its *kaupang* market towns, fits here naturally.
- **It sits between slot A and the lush coast (slot 1).** Either could be its county, or it could be a chartered town of its own.

**2. The capital can't feed itself by water.** *(Follows from the map.)*
The capital in the crook has only a short stream and a small bay. The farmland that could feed it (slots D and F, the plains) drains *south*, to the estuary. The sea route from the estuary to the capital runs all the way round the Levont's eastern tip: about 1,300 km, or 9 to 13 days of sailing. The straight line overland is about 130 km.
- **So the first railroad line almost certainly ran from the capital to the estuary.** Four or five hours by train against a week and a half by sea.
- **The capital is the Grainbound's "parasite city",** living on grain that comes up the rails. Cut that line and the capital is hungry within weeks. That makes the line the council's lifeline, and the most valuable target in the Levont.

**3. The Veingold hold the headwaters.** *(Follows from the map.)*
The Basin River and the Marsh River both rise in the Veingold uplands, and so do the salt valley's streams. The Grainbound's point about rivers is that whoever holds the sources has leverage over everyone downstream. The Veingold already hold everyone's debts. Now they hold the water too: dams, mill rights, timber floated downstream, and mine waste that could poison a river.

**4. The main gate sits at the head of a river valley.** *(Follows from the map.)*
Pass roads follow rivers, so an army through the gate comes straight down the Plains River valley onto the plains and toward the estuary. The same river carries supplies *up* to the March, slowly, against the current. That's why the plains county takes the first blow ([[World Scale]]).

**5. The marshes are a delta.**
The Marsh River's delta explains why the north-central coast is wet, why elves built cities there in the Elden Era, and why its people would know boats and waterways. It also suggests an answer to the crows (Area 4): in a delta full of channels and fog, a messenger bird is faster than any boat.

**6. The salt valley has no outlet.** *(Decided 24 Oct.)* For a lake with no outlet to stay salty, the valley must be dry: the Veingold uplands to the west catch the rain, leaving the valley in their rain shadow, and hills ring it on every side with no gap lower than the lake. The steppe is then dry by nature as well as salted by the war. Real model: Lake Neusiedl on the Austria–Hungary border, a shallow, slightly salty steppe lake at about the same latitude, which dried out completely in the 1860s.
If the streams in the salt valley end in a lake with no way out, the water evaporates and leaves salt, which is how real salt pans form. The steppe would then be salty for two reasons: the war salted it, and the land was already collecting salt. The Salthollow clan (*Zaldukhoz*, "salt-pit folk", [[Dwarves]]) worked the dragons' salt pits, so they would know exactly what to do with it.

**7. The divide is a natural border.**
North of the divide (the heartland, G, J, the marshes) water runs north. South of it (the plains, D, F, the lush coast, the estuary) water runs south. If the old countries' borders followed it, the heartland and the river-trade country were neighbours on either side of one watershed. The counties, drawn across the old borders on purpose, would then cut across the divide ([[Counties of the Levont]]).

## The channels (26 Oct)
Dwarves cut channels along the old elven rivers during the ascension war, without asking the elves. See [[The Channels]]. One thing to watch: a working channel across the main divide would let the capital be fed by water, which undoes point 2 above. Broken or silted channels keep the railroad vital.

## Names
**The rivers keep Elvish names** (decided 22 Oct), the oldest layer of names on the map ([[Languages and Naming]]).

**Why, in the real world.** River names are the oldest place names in England. Many come from Brittonic, the Celtic language spoken before English, and a few may be older still. *Avon* comes from Brittonic *abona*, which simply means "river", so "River Avon" means "river river". River names survive for a reason: everyone along a river uses it every day for water, fish and transport, and it runs past many villages, so no single newcomer can rename it. New lords rename towns; they rarely rename rivers. Each new people learns the name from the people already living there.

**Why it fits the Levont.**
- **The elves were here first.** In the Elden Era there were elven cities in the marshes, on the Marsh River's delta, and the [[Aelnor]] kingdom had cities in the middle and north.
- **Elves live long.** Names given by a people who live for centuries had time to settle before the human old countries rose.
- **The humans learned them second-hand,** the way the English learned Brittonic names. So the names wore down in human mouths, the way *Eburākon* became *York* ([Worldbuilding: The Levont](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/7901a3cd-349a-4a4a-95b2-b4078f9db570)).

**What it gives you:**
- **Tautologies.** A river called "the Abhainn River" when *abhainn* already meant river, just like the Avon.
- **Worn-down names.** A long Elvish name shortened to one or two syllables by farmers who never knew what it meant.
- **People who still know.** Aelnor elders who say the names correctly and know their meaning, and Lurian's archivists who keep the old forms. To them every river name is a reminder that the elves were here first.
- **Names as politics.** An Aelnor group that wants the kingdom restored could insist on the full Elvish forms. Using the worn-down names in the common tongue would be a statement too.

**Names (approved 22 Oct),** built from the Elvish roots in [[Languages and Naming]], with the worn-down form the Levont's humans use:

| # | Working name | Elvish | Meaning | Worn-down human form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Plains River | *Mahelavenn* | plain river | the Mallon |
| 2 | Basin River | *Dovainavenn* | deep river | the Dovan |
| 3 | Marsh River | *Riasavenn* | marsh river | the Rissan |
| 4 | Capital stream | *Solavenán* | little river of light | the Solvan |
| 5 | G river | *Préanavenn* | crow river | the Pren |
| 6 | Salt pan | *Deorloen* | lake of tears | Darlon, or the Tearmere |
| 7 | Valley River | *Aiselavenn* | dream river (in the valley where no one sleeps) | the Aislan |
| 8 | The estuary | *Cumar* | where rivers meet | Comber |

Humans who don't know *avenn* means river say "the Mallon River": a river-river, just like the Avon. The salt lake as "tears" ties it to the Martyr's tears, a reading the Martyr's church would like.
