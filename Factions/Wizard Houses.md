# Wizard Houses

Noble families descended from the apprentices and trusted servants of the wizard-lords. Added 12 Nov 2026. The working name "wizard houses" is a placeholder until the naming pass.

## Decided (12 Nov 2026)
- **Before [[The Parting]] there were no schools of magic.** Some wizard-lords kept apprentices and lesser wizards they had taught; some kept none. It depended on each lord's personality, on both sides of [[The Sundering]]. You learned magic only if your "god" favoured you, or you found the books.
- **After the Parting, the lines of those apprentices and close henchmen became nobles: wizard nobles.** Some banded together to found prestigious academies of magic with restricted access.
- **In the Levont they hold no land.** They have homes, money and prestige, and that gives them leverage in society.
- **Blood or books:** the houses say blood is what fuels magic. It is obviously the books. **Anyone can become a wizard**, but it is costly, hard and rigorous. A child of a wizard house, such as the **Arklau** family, is expected to already be above other wizards.
- **The losers' apprentices fled to the [[The Veiled Frontier|Veiled Frontier]]** to hide. Their descendants now rule small kingdoms there: natural villains, and sponsors of evildoers. Some Frontier dukes are wizard nobles.
- **The Larhouse name is dropped, and it is not the only licensed school.** There are at least two licensed schools in the Levont. The Frontier has more places to learn, which mages know of and ordinary people don't care to learn about.

## Suggestions (not canon)
From [Wizard Houses: Proposal](https://claude.ai/artifact/KVu1wNMxKN9URgyxtxH6dW).
- **Why they are respected:** the gods never walk the world, so the apprentices were the last people a god taught face to face. Nobody alive can check that claim.
- **Legitimacy** ([[Worldbuilding Sage Lessons]], Weber): charismatic made traditional ("our founder was taught by a god"), against the council's legal licence and the great houses' land.
- **The licence is the fight:** academies that accept the council's licence keep their privileges; those that refuse go private or move to the Frontier.
- **A century ago:** before the union (about 120 years ago) the houses served the old kingdoms as court mages. The railroad (about 50 years ago) needed more shard-fitters than the houses would teach.
- **The other side:** landed great houses see rich upstarts without land; clergy call arcane magic theft from the gods; the desirenthropy faithful see the people who got through the door before it was locked.
- **Folklore:** each house keeps a "first lesson", the words its founder heard from a god, and every house's version differs. Saying: "God-taught, money-kept." Commoners say a wizard-house child is born with ink on their fingers.
- **Hooks:** a reborn elf remembers being taught by [[Athréth]]'s own hand and claims a place in an academy that admits only the old lines; a wizard house buys a county's debts, and the council finds it owes a family with no land; a Frontier wizard duke, heir of the losing side, writes politely asking for his ancestor's books back.
- **Borrowing check:** close to *Dragon Age*'s Tevinter magisters, but those rule; ours are landless in the Levont. Hereditary noble magic echoes *Mistborn* (deliberate). "It's in the blood" snobbery echoes *Harry Potter*, which is why the truth is books. To score in [[Borrowed Ideas]].
