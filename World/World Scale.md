# World Scale

How big Beredluinn is, how long it takes to get anywhere, and roughly how many people live there. **The scale is canon (decided 22 October 2026).** The latitude, climate and population figures below are still suggestions. All numbers were measured from the 22 October map draft and calculated, not guessed. Answers **14.12** in [[Worldbuilding Gaps]].

![[Sleepless Lands scale map.jpg]]

## The scale
**The whole map is about 2,850 km on each side.** In pixels:

| Copy of the map | Kilometres per pixel | 100 km is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full Krita size (18,439 px) | 0.155 km (155 m) | about 647 px |
| A 4,000 px export | 0.71 km | about 140 px |
| A 1,900 px copy | 1.5 km | about 67 px |

For any other export: **kilometres per pixel = 2,850 ÷ the image's width in pixels.** The same number goes into the Leaflet plugin's scale setting when the map becomes interactive.

## Why this scale
It was picked so the things you've already written fit, not the other way round.
- **The Levont comes out about the size of the United Kingdom** (about 246,000 km², 840 km west to east, 510 km north to south). That's big enough for five old countries and six counties, and small enough to feel like one realm.
- **The March is about 210 km long.** The Offa's Dyke Path, which loosely follows the real England–Wales border, is 285 km. Your Welsh Marches model fits almost exactly.
- **The council can only reach the heartland on its own.** The Grainbound's three-day rule says a ruler governs about 100 km over land ([[Grainbound Lessons]]). From the capital, that covers slots D, F, G, J and the marshes, and little else. Every other county is too far to rule directly without the railroad. That's your "strong counties, weak council" falling straight out of the geography.
- **Each county averages about 41,000 km²**, a radius of about 115 km. That's a little more than one lord can run directly, so every count needs sub-lords: the middle layer from [[Land, Lordship and Church]].
- **The railroad turns a month into two days.** Walking across the Levont takes about four weeks; the train takes about two days. That's why the rails are the council's main lever ([[The Railroad]]).

**If you want it bigger or smaller:** double the km per pixel and every distance doubles and every area quadruples. At 1 km per pixel the Levont is about England's size (110,000 km²). At 2 km per pixel it's about Sweden's (440,000 km²), and walking across it takes two months.

## Suggested latitude and climate
Suggestion only. With the Levont's capital at about 48°N (like southern Germany or Paris), the map runs from about 32°N at the bottom to about 59°N at the top, a slice of world roughly like Europe from North Africa to southern Norway. The blue lines on the scale map show it.
- **The northeast continent (53 to 59°N):** cold, conifer forest and bog. Suits the [[Northern Elves]].
- **Lorweir (49 to 56°N):** a cold, dry steppe inside its mountain ring. Ulaanbaatar is at 48°N.
- **The Levont (45 to 50°N):** temperate, like France or Germany. Wheat, rye, oats, orchards.
- **The Veiled Frontier and Adaria (38 to 46°N):** warmer, like Italy or the Balkans, drier toward the south.
- **The southern continent (32 to 36°N):** Mediterranean to subtropical.
- **Winds:** if they blow from the west, as in Europe, the March's western slopes are wet and forested and the Levont's interior is partly in their rain shadow.

**Indigo needs warmth.** True indigo is a tropical plant. Colonial Georgia and South Carolina, at about 32 to 34°N, grew it commercially. Medieval northern Europe dyed blue with woad instead. So the [[The Indigo Outpost|Indigo Outpost]] belongs on a warm coast, such as the southern continent's, which would explain why it's "miles away" from home. Alternatively the Levont grows woad at home and the outpost's indigo is the expensive import that undercuts it.

## Travel speeds
| How | Per day | Source or reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On foot, on roads | 30 km | Medieval records give up to 30 miles (48 km) for fit walkers; 30 km allows for ordinary people and weather |
| Wagon or ox cart | 20 km | Loaded carts were the slowest traffic on medieval roads |
| Army | 16 km | Bret Devereaux: sustained pre-modern armies made about 8 to 12 miles (13 to 19 km) a day |
| Rider | 55 km | 30 to 40 miles a day was normal on horseback; above 40 wasn't sustainable |
| Courier with fresh horses | 90 km | Urgent royal messengers managed 52 to 56 miles a day |
| Train | 400 km | **Assumption:** about 40 km/h for ten hours, with stops |
| Off-road, hills or forest | Halve it | |
| Mountains | A quarter | |

Roads wind, so road distance is about 1.3 times the straight line.

**Other useful distances:**

## Why the size matters: answering an attack
**Decided 22 Oct:** the size is chosen so that an attack on the paths into the Levont *can* be answered, but mustering forces and moving them is a real problem. The numbers bear that out. Without the railroad, a full answer to an attack on the March takes something like this:

| Step | How long | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The alarm reaches the capital | Hours | The beacon chain along the March and inland |
| The summons reaches the counties | 2 to 6 days | Couriers with fresh horses cover about 90 km a day. The far east is 600 km away by road |
| Each county gathers its levy | 1 to 3 weeks | Farmers have to be called in, armed and fed, and harvest time makes it worse |
| The levies march to the gate | 3 days to 5 weeks | The plains are 3 days from the gate. The capital's troops need about 19 days, the eastern counties over a month |
| **A full Levont army at the gate** | **About 4 to 8 weeks after the alarm** | |

What follows from it:
- **The March must hold alone for a month or more.** That's why the keeps need stores for a siege, and why the marcher lords get liberties nobody else has.
- **The plains county takes the first blow.** It's three days from the gate, so its garrisons, constables and twice-yearly parade make sense: it's the only county that can't wait for the others.
- **Whoever holds the railroad holds the answer.** A trunk line toward the March cuts weeks to days. That makes the rails the council's most important military asset, and the first target for anyone planning an invasion.
- **Harvest and winter decide the timing.** An attack at harvest catches the levies at their slowest, so enemies who know the Levont will choose their season.

## How many people (estimate)
A first estimate for the Levont, using medieval England as the check: about 4 to 5 million people around 1300, on 130,000 km², with about 10% living in towns by 1377.

| Land | Share of the Levont | People per km² | People |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Farmland and pasture | 50% | 40 | 4.9 million |
| Forest, preserves and hills | 15% | 8 | 300,000 |
| Mountains | 20% | 3 | 150,000 |
| Marsh | 7% | 10 | 170,000 |
| Salt steppe | 8% | 4 | 80,000 |
| **Total** | | **about 23 average** | **about 5.6 million** |

The land shares are rough guesses from the map, to correct once terrain is drawn. What follows from about 5.6 million:
- **About 90% farm.** That's roughly 20,000 to 25,000 villages of 200 to 250 people, most never drawn on the map ([[Fantasy Forge Lessons]]).
- **About 10% live in towns:** around 550,000 townspeople.
- **The capital:** 50,000 to 80,000 if the railroad and farm slots D and F feed it. That's a metropolis by the Fantasy Forge's measure. Without the rails, cap it nearer 20,000 to 30,000.
- **County seats:** 8,000 to 20,000 each. **Market towns:** 40 to 60 of them, 1,000 to 5,000 people.
- **A full field army:** medieval realms put about 0.2 to 0.5% of their people into one army. Estimates for Edward III's army at Crécy (1346) mostly fall between about 7,000 and 15,000 men, from a kingdom of 4 to 5 million. For the Levont that's about **11,000 to 28,000 soldiers**, all the counties and the Order together.

**The horde for comparison.** Steppe land feeds far fewer people, about 1 or 2 per km². Lorweir's basin would then hold 200,000 to 400,000 hobgoblins and others. Nomads put almost every adult man on a horse, about one rider for every five or six people. That gives **35,000 to 75,000 riders**, more than the whole Levont can field. That's a serious threat on its own, and a good answer to 2.4: the pressure that made the old countries unite. How fast hobgoblins breed changes these numbers, and that's yours to decide.
