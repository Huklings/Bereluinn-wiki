# Death and the Afterlife

How death, souls and resurrection work. Several gods and peoples have a piece of it.

## What we know
- [[The Smiling Father]] meets the dead. Souls go to "whatever god has claimed them", which may mean into that god's realm ([[The Tapestries]]).
- [[The Bud and the Sprout]] allow only new-body resurrection.
- [[The Sunturner]] has Rebuke Death.
- [[Lusitri]]'s Soul domain speaks to the dead.
- [[Dwarves]] cremate their dead and keep the ashes (the Dolumar), possibly as protection against undead.
- Fate: even gods must eventually pass on.
- **Elves can be resurrected**, except the [[Northern Elves]], who lost this by joining the betrayer gods and seek lichdom and undeath instead.
- Souls in a god's tapestry are slowly changed by it. [[Angels]] are souls mutated this way.

## Elves are reborn (decided 26 Oct)
- **Elves live about 400 years.** That is a limit, not a natural span: the god who made them had to make them die after a while.
- **Instead, elves are constantly reborn.** Each elf child is the incarnation of a dead elf.
- **Their numbers are capped.** A child can only be born when an elf soul is waiting for rebirth, and with their long lives that is rare.
- **Some stronger souls remember** their past lives, but are often their own person. Others get only figments of past memories and experiences.
- **Elves know rebirth memory exists**, but it is hard to explain, and hard to prove or disprove.
- Inspiration: Tolkien's elves returning, like Glorfindel. Yours are reborn as children who are partly someone new. Scored in [[Borrowed Ideas]].

### What follows (suggestions, not canon)
- **Elves hate spending lives.** Every elf killed leaves a gap until they're reborn, so elves are reluctant to go to war. That explains a lot about how they'd act on the council.
- **A grudge kept by teaching.** No living elf saw [[The Channels]] cut, so the grievance lives in songs, teaching and lineage, which fits the Gaelic side of Elvish ([[Languages and Naming]]).
- **Claimed memories are power.** An elf who says they remember can shape elven policy, and nobody can easily prove them wrong. That gives the elves an internal conflict of their own.

### To check
- **Resurrection and rebirth.** The vault says elves can be resurrected, except the [[Northern Elves]]. With a fixed pool of souls, does resurrection pull a soul back out of the queue?
- **The northern elves lost rebirth too (decided 26 Oct).** Instead they use necromancy to draw the soul out of the body and bind it to a body, much like lichdom ([[Northern Elves]]).
- **Who made the elves?** The gods are the wizards of old, who ascended. If the elves' maker was one of them, were there elves before the ascension? See [[Elven Pantheon]] and [[Timeline#The Ascension War (open, 26 Oct)]].
- **Does it apply to every elf?** [[Aelnor]], [[Finelor]] and the northern elves.

## Souls can change hands (decided 2 Nov)
- **A soul goes to the gods its owner turns to.** Anyone, of any people, who starts worshipping another pantheon can have their soul claimed by those gods. Since 2 Nov, "bargain" means an oath kept by [[Verdan Ironoath]]; the soul itself goes where its worship painted it (see below). A dwarf who turns away from [[Ishgadrum]] can be lost to him. So humans are not the only ones open to this.
- **Humans were given the "freedom" to choose more easily.** The other peoples have churches for their own pantheons, which hold them close: dwarves to their maker's halls, elves to rebirth, the Wendfolk to their hearth. Humans have no such church of their own, so their souls are the easiest to win, and gods compete for them. The quotation marks are deliberate: a freedom that makes you everyone's prize.

### What follows (suggestions, not canon)
- ~~Deathbed bargains~~ (rejected 2 Nov: souls are never traded; see How a soul finds its god).
- **Turning is a scandal among the other peoples.** A dwarf who takes another god risks losing their place on the ash wall; an elf who does may never be reborn, which other elves would see as a theft from the whole people (compare trapped souls below).
- **Do the old claims fight back?** Ishgadrum, the elven gods and Greenhand may try to win a lost soul back, which gives the gods quiet wars over single souls.
- **Who made humans?** Still open. A maker who gave them no church of their own is a story in itself ([[The Smiling Father]] was proposed, not chosen).

## How a soul finds its god (decided 2 Nov)
- **[[The Smiling Father]] walks every soul.** He takes the place other settings give a judge of the dead, but he does not judge and has no restrictions. He walks each soul to the tapestry of the god that lays claim to it.
- **Worship paints the soul.** "Painted" works like rubbing against something and getting its colour on your skin. A farmer who follows the gods of their parish all their life, keeping their creed, edicts and anathema, is painted by that worship. A life can carry the colours of several gods.
- **The Smiling Father knows.** At death he looks upon the soul, knows which god it belongs to, and walks it to that god's tapestry.
- **A binding oath dunks the soul** (decided 2 Nov). Swearing yourself to a god can happen in many ways, and [[Verdan Ironoath]] writes down and keeps every oath. If he judges an oath binding, the soul is not just rubbed against that god's tapestry: it is *dunked* into it. The god will most likely grant the oath-taker power, and the soul is far, far more claimed by that god than any lifetime of ordinary worship would make it.
- **Example:** a desperate man prays for strength to win a battle. His desperation opens him to a god of slaughter and mayhem, and he says "Please help me win this battle, and I will worship." Ironoath can take that as a binding oath. The man wins, perhaps with power that isn't his, and his soul is dunked.
- **An oath binds only once the god has answered** (decided 2 Nov). Until the god grants the mortal some aid, it is only words. Once it does, it is a contract and an oath. The aid usually comes as power from the god's domain: domain spells and other boons, perhaps strength.
- **A dunked soul can be washed** (decided 2 Nov), in three ways:
    - the god releases it (rare);
    - a binding oath to another god dunks it again, though that god may refuse to aid someone already dunked by another;
    - a lifetime of worship slowly repaints it.
- **Saying (canon):** "Dye takes a day, washing takes a life."
- **Humans are born uncoloured** (decided 2 Nov). Every other people is born already coloured by its maker (dwarves by [[Ishgadrum]], elves by [[Athréth]], Wendfolk by [[Greenhand]]). Humans alone are born with a blank soul. They never had a single maker: several makers made them, and chose together to leave their souls uncoloured. See [[Humans]].
- **The first touch** (folklore, decided 2 Nov). Within days of a birth, a parent presses the newborn against a shrine or a priest's hand to give it a first colour, since a baby who dies blank only stays with the Smiling Father a while before fading. Poor or remote families who can't reach a shrine have their own hedge customs.
- This is how humans' "freedom" works in practice: with no church of their own maker holding them, humans are painted by whatever they worship, and gods compete for their devotion, not for a price.

### What follows (suggestions, not canon)
- **Folklore:** "You're painted by what you kneel to." Some old folk refuse to pray to anything on their last day, so they "go as they were".
- **Folklore:** "Mind your *if*s on the field." Soldiers' saying: never bargain with a god in battle, because Ironoath is listening. Sergeants slap recruits who start a prayer with "if you help me".
- **Folklore:** at a funeral one chair is left empty "for whoever comes", since nobody is sure which god the soul was painted for.
- **Contested souls.** A god whose oath is in Ironoath's book may feel cheated when the soul goes elsewhere. Gods argue it out between themselves, at a distance, never with mortals in person.
- **Folklore:** "Spoken, meant, answered." Children chant it like a rhyme for what makes an oath bind.
- **Forced oaths:** an oath forced on someone by another mortal is written down by Ironoath but never binds, since it was not meant.
- **The old god notices** when a soul is being washed; the power it granted may fade or turn.
- **A washing order:** a temple order whose calling is helping the dunked wash clean, resented by the gods they wash away from.
- **Hooks:** a soldier who won an impossible fight and now hears a god of slaughter in his sleep, looking for a way to wash the dye off; a Reikhart lord makes his household swear to his patron, but he fears their hearts were elsewhere; a deathbed convert whose priest can't tell if the paint took; a soul painted equally for two gods, which the Smiling Father has not yet walked anywhere.

### Open

## Souls can be trapped (26 Oct)
- **Decided:** souls can be trapped, and elves may want to free souls trapped by several things not yet decided.
- *Suggestion:* a trapped elf soul can never be reborn. To the elves that is the worst crime there is, since the people lose a member forever.
- *Suggestion:* the Oathbound's jade name-stones hold dwarf spirits, and Ashwall steel holds fragments of the dead ([[Dwarves]]). If the elves free *any* trapped soul, not only elven ones, they would see both as prisons, while the dwarves see them as honour.
- *Suggestion:* toad alchemy is another candidate ([[Toad People]]).
