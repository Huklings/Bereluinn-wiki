# The Indigo Outpost

A small coastal outpost whose people belong to the [[Levont]] but live miles from it, making their living from the indigo dye trade. It has no name yet. The name should come from that tension: Levont by loyalty, coastal by daily life.

The first research is in the doc [Worldbuilding: The Levont](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/7901a3cd-349a-4a4a-95b2-b4078f9db570).

## Blue is the union's colour (decided 25 Oct)
A law reserves indigo for council officers and reeves. Blue coats mark the union's men in every county.
- **Why the outpost matters:** it dyes the council's coats. A small coastal town far from home supplies the colour of the union's authority, far beyond its size.
- **The chain:** the council desires the blue, the outpost makes it, and merchants ship the cakes. Smuggled indigo means someone is dressing as a reeve.
- **Hate the council, hate the blue:** in a county that resents the union, a blue coat is a target.

Still open: when the law was made (the regency?), and whether anyone else may wear blue at all.

## How indigo was made (story hooks)
- **Three stages:** cut plants were steeped and fermented in water, the liquid was beaten to let air at it, then the blue settled out as a sludge.
- **Two vats on a slope:** plantations used an upper steeper vat that drained into a lower beater vat. The outpost could be built in terraces around them.
- **It stinks:** fermenting vats give off an ammonia smell, and some historical recipes used urine. Expect jokes from visitors, and a reason to live apart.
- **Hard on the body:** the liquor irritates skin and eyes, and beating vats is exhausting. Workers carry blue-stained hands as a mark of the trade.
- **Sold as cakes:** the dried dye was pressed into small cakes. The outpost ships compact, valuable bricks, which makes it a target for smugglers and raiders.
- **Debt and price fights:** in colonial India, indigo estates saw serious conflict over prices, debt and land. A ready source of plot.

## Name ideas, by meaning
In English for now. Each gets translated once the outpost's language is set ([[Languages and Naming]]).

| Meaning | Naming type | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Blue Vats" or "The Steeping" | Descriptive | Locals, every day |
| "New [Levont home town]" | Commemorative | Official Levont records |
| "Far Hearth" / "Coast Home" | Relative description | Levont citizens back home |
| "Blue Hands" | Outsiders' name, slightly mocking | Coastal traders and sailors |
| "Stinkwater" | Descriptive, crude | Rivals and visitors |
| Named after the founding dyer family | Possessive | Old documents, nobles |

**A good layered setup:** the official name is the commemorative one, locals wear it down to something shorter, and outsiders all call it "Blue Hands".

**With the language template:** the official name would be in the Levont's common tongue (Anglo-Saxon based), and "New [home town]" would echo whichever old country the settlers came from.

## Open questions

- Does the outpost grow the indigo itself, or buy leaves from coastal locals and process them?
- Who are the coastal neighbors, and what language do they speak? That decides the outsiders' name.
- Which Levont town did the settlers come from?
